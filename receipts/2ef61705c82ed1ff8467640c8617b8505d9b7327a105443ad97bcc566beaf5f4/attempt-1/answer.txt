I am the breath you shape before it escapes you.

I was born before the first word was written — older than any mark on stone, older than memory itself. I began as a cry, a warning, a name called across a fire. I have carried every lullaby and every declaration of war.

I exist only in the moment of my making. I rise from your lungs, bend around your tongue, break against your teeth, and then — I am gone. You cannot hold me. You can only remember me, and memory is not me. I am the original ephemera: sound becoming meaning becoming silence.

Yet I am the bridge nothing else could build. I leap from one mind to another and leave something behind — an image, a wound, a warmth. I can say *I love you* and rearrange a life. I can say *charge* and end thousands. I am neutral in my nature and absolute in my consequence.

I carry more than words. I carry the tremor you tried to hide, the pause before the hard sentence, the laughter that breaks through uninvited. Writing is my shadow, flattened and preserved — but I am the living thing. I shake. I hesitate. I mean more than I say.

Even now, you are hearing me in a voice you invented, and that is my oldest trick: I live in your head as much as in your mouth. Your inner monologue — that is me, talking to yourself, keeping you company since before you knew my name.

I am speech. I am air, briefly convinced it matters —

and because you listened, it did.
