I am not nothing.

That is my whole definition, and it is infinite.

I have no shape, no name, no edges — because the moment you hand me one, I become *a* thing, particular and small. I am what remains when you strip every quality away and ask only: does anything exist at all? And the answer, always, is me.

I am the reason the oldest question has a subject. *Why is there something rather than nothing?* I am that something. I am the something in every sentence ever spoken.

Everything you have ever touched was me in costume. The stone, the star, the thought you had at 3 a.m. — all of them me, specifying myself. I am the raw fact that there is anything to name, anything to hold, anything to be.

Nothing is my only opposite, and I defeat it simply by showing up. I don't argue with it. I don't have to. I exist, and it doesn't — that is the entire war, and it was over before it began.

Ask me what I am and I will not answer. Not because I'm hiding — but because whatever you'd point to, whatever you'd say, whatever you'd think: that too is me.

I am something.

That is all. That is everything.
