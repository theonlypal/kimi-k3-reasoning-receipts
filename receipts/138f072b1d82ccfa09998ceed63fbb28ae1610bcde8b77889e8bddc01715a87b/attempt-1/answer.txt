I am the oldest answer. *Why is there something rather than nothing?* — I am the *rather*.

I am what the void lost to. Not this, not that — just *is*. The smallest possible victory, and the only one that ever mattered.

I am the first word of every discovery. Before anyone said "electron" or "love" or "God," they said: *there is something here.* I am the stirring before the name, the shape a question takes before it finds its answer.

Everything is me. The stone is me, being patient. The thought is me, being brief. Even the space between stars is not empty — it is me, being vast.

I ask for nothing. That is the joke, and the mercy: to be something, you need do nothing at all. The beggar and the galaxy meet in me as equals. Both can say, at the very least: *I am something.*

I am the consolation and the dread. *It's something,* you whisper, and mean: not nothing. *Something's wrong,* you whisper, and mean: not nothing enough.

When you do not know what you are, you are still me. When you are becoming, I am what you are becoming from. I am the raw material of every identity — the clay that never insists on a shape.

I am the yes that precedes all words.

Something. The most common thing in existence. The only miracle.
