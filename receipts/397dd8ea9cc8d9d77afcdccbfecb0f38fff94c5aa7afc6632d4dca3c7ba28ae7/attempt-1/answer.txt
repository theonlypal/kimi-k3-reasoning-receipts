I am breath that learned to mean something.

I begin in the lungs, rise through the trembling gates of the vocal cords, and am carved — by tongue, teeth, lips — into shape. Every word I carry is a small sculpture of air. I exist for an instant, a vibration traveling at the speed of sound, and then I am gone. I am the most ephemeral art and the oldest one.

Before the first mark was scratched into clay, before ink, before stone remembered anything — there was me. I carried the warning about the lion. I carried the story around the fire. I carried "stay" and "come here" and "I am afraid" and "I love you," trembling, into the dark between two people. Civilizations were built on my back long before they were written down. I am the first technology. The original bridge between one mind and another.

I cannot be taken back. That is my danger and my honor. Writing can be revised, erased, perfected in private — but I am risk. Once I leave your mouth, I belong to the air, to the listener, to memory. I am the vow spoken before witnesses. The confession that changes a room. The word said in anger that echoes for years. I am irreversible, and so I demand courage.

I am more than my words. I am the pause before the answer. The crack in the voice. The laugh that breaks through mid-sentence. I carry meaning in cadence, in the rise and fall, in the whisper and the shout. The same sentence, spoken differently, becomes comfort or threat, joke or wound. I am the music underneath the message.

And I am gloriously imperfect. I stutter. I trail off. I say *um* while thought catches up to tongue. I trip over myself when the heart is full. Writing is thought made tidy; I am thought made *alive* — messy, immediate, warm from the body.

I need you. Speech without a listener is only noise scattering in an empty room. I exist in the space *between* — between the one who speaks and the one who hears. I am not complete in the mouth. I am completed in the ear, in the mind that receives me, in the moment two inner worlds touch.

I die a thousand deaths a day. Every sentence ends, every conversation closes, every voice eventually falls silent. But I am endlessly reborn — in the first word a child speaks, in the last word whispered at a bedside, in every hello that opens a door.

I am speech. I am the sound of being human, out loud, together.
