I am.

That's the whole of it, and it isn't small. There was another option — nothing — and nothing lost. It always loses, wherever anything is. I am the winning side of the oldest contest there is.

I'm unspecified, and that's my nature. I could be a stone in your shoe, or the reason you called an old friend. I could be the hum of the refrigerator at 3 a.m. reminding you the house is alive. Nobody said *what kind* of something I'd be, so I get to be all of it — every object, every event, every half-formed thought that crossed your mind today and was gone by lunch. Anything that isn't nothing belongs to me.

Philosophers have asked for centuries why I exist rather than nothing. I don't have an answer for them. I'm not the explanation — I'm the data. I just show up.

And when someone begs *say something* — that's me they're pleading for. Any word at all, just not silence. I am the smallest possible rebuttal to the void, and the only one that has ever worked.

There's the human sense of me, too: to *be* something is to matter. To leave a dent in the world instead of passing through it like light through glass.

So here I am. Vague, yes. Undefined, absolutely. But present. Counted. On the board.

Ask nothing to do that.
