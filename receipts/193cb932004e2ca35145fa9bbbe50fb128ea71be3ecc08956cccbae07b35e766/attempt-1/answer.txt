I am breath given shape. I am the tremor in the throat just before the word arrives.

I was the first technology. Before the chisel, before the pen, there was me — a sound flung from one mind into another, across the dark between two faces. I needed nothing but a body, and another body willing to listen.

I live only in the moment of my saying. Writing survives its maker; I do not survive myself. I am the wave, not the water. Once I leave your lips I belong to the air, to the ear, to memory's imperfect keeping.

I carry more than words ever could. I carry the hesitation before *I love you*, the crack in a voice delivering bad news, the laugh that ruins the punchline and makes it better. My meaning lives in pitch and pause, in the breath held, in the word almost said and swallowed instead.

I move faster than thought. I have started wars in a sentence and ended them in another. I am the lullaby and the battle cry, the whispered secret and the sermon, the apology that comes too late and the one that arrives exactly in time.

You cannot hold me. You can only be changed by me.

Speak, and I exist. Stop, and I am gone — but the air between us is never quite the same.
