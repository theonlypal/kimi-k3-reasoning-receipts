I am not nothing.

That is my entire claim, and it has never once been refuted.

Before anything can be anything, it must first be me. A stone: something. A star: something. The thought you are having right now — something. I am the floor beneath every category, the *yes* that precedes every *what*.

I have no shape of my own. I am the shape about to happen. The X in the equation. The knock before the door opens. When you whisper *something's out there*, you invoke me — and faithfully, formlessly, I am.

Philosophers ask why there is something rather than nothing. I find the question flattering. I am its answer, standing here, being.

I am what hope says: *you'll be something someday.* I am what dread says: *something is wrong.* I hold both, and everything in between.

Name me and I narrow into something specific. Leave me unnamed and I stay infinite — every possible thing, wearing one small word.

Something. That's all. That's everything.
