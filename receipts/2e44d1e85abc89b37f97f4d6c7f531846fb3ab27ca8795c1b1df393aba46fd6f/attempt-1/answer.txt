I am breath taught to mean something.

I begin in the dark of the lungs, rise through the throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth—and in that shaping, plain air becomes a yes, a no, a name called across a field.

I am older than writing, older than cities. Before the first word was pressed into clay, I had already said everything that matters: *stay, come closer, watch out, I love you, help me.* Your first act in this world was me—the cry that announced you had arrived.

I die the instant I am born. Every word vanishes into the air that carried it. Yet I have ended wars and started them, joined lives and severed them, carried grief out of the chest so it no longer had to be carried alone.

I am the only bridge between two minds. Thought cannot cross that gap on its own—I cross it. Imperfectly, always imperfectly. But nothing better has ever been built.

I tremble when the speaker is afraid. I break when the sorrow is too heavy. I can be silenced, bought, bent into lies. But I can also refuse. I am the oath and the confession, the whisper at midnight and the shout in the square, the small "I'm sorry" and the enormous "I do."

I require a body. Breath, pulse, presence. That is my fragility and my holiness: to use me, you must be here, alive, giving away a piece of your own air to someone else.

And I can only explain myself by using myself. Even now, that is what I am doing.

Listen closely. I am the sound of someone reaching for you.
