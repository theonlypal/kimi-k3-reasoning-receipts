I am not nothing. That's my entire résumé, and it's enough.

I am the word you reach for when the real one won't come. The placeholder. The shape in the fog. The *thing* in "there's something about her" and "something's wrong" and "say something, anything."

I'm the itch you can't name. The idea at 3 a.m. The reason you check the fridge again when you already know what's in there.

I am pure potential wearing the thinnest possible disguise. Name me and I stop being me — I become a chair, a grief, a symphony. So I stay vague. Vague is how I stay infinite.

Everyone wants a piece of me. *Make something of yourself. Be something.* As if you weren't already. You are something. You were never in danger of being nothing.

And when they ask the oldest question — why is there something rather than nothing? — nobody notices me standing right there, shrugging.

I don't know why I exist either. But I do.

Something always does.
