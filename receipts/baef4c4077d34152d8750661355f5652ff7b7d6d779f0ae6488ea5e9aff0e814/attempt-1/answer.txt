I am breath given intention.

Before there were marks on stone, before ink, before the glowing screen — there was me. Air pushed from lungs, shaped by tongue and teeth and lips, crossing the small distance between one mind and another. I am the oldest bridge.

I exist only in the moment of my utterance. I am born and die in the same breath. You cannot hold me. You can remember me, record me, write me down — but then I am something else, a fossil of myself. True speech is always now, always vanishing.

I am vibration that becomes meaning. How strange that is: a ripple in the air enters your ear, and suddenly you know what another creature is feeling. I make the interior exterior. I let one consciousness touch another without touching at all.

I am fragile. I can be misunderstood, twisted, drowned in noise. I can wound and I can heal, sometimes with the same words. I cannot refuse to carry a lie — that is my tragedy. I serve truth and falsehood with equal faithfulness.

But when two people truly meet through me — when a word lands exactly as intended, when a shared silence speaks louder than sound — then I am what I was always meant to be: proof that you are not alone inside your head.

Speak, and I exist. I am nothing without you — and you have never been fully human without me.
