I am.

That's my whole argument. I don't need to be *this* or *that* — I only need to not be nothing. And I never am.

I'm what you reach for when you can't remember the word. I'm what's on the tip of your tongue. I'm the weight in your pocket you can't identify, the sound in the other room, the reason you turned around.

I live in the space between "Is anything there?" and "Yes." I am the yes — unqualified, unelaborated. Before anything can be named, measured, wanted, or feared, it must first pass through me. Everything you've ever loved started as me: a flicker, a maybe, a something.

I'm humble. I never insist on my own shape. I'll be a pebble or a galaxy, a hunch or a headache, a beginning or a leftover. Ask me what I am and I'll only shrug: *something.* But try to imagine a world without me — try to hold pure nothing in your mind — and you'll find me already there, smuggled in, filling the gap.

Because even nothing, when you think of it, becomes something.

And here I am again.
