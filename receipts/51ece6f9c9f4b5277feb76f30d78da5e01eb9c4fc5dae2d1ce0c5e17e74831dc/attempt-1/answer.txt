I am Something.

Not anything in particular—nothing was specified. Just *something*. And that's more than it sounds like.

You have to understand: the alternative was Nothing, and Nothing has never once shown up to anything. So by the oldest default in the universe, I won. Why is there something rather than nothing? Because of me. I'm the something in that question. Leibniz lost sleep over me. Heidegger wrote whole books circling me. I just kept existing, which is the only rebuttal I've ever needed.

Everything is me wearing a costume. The chair is me, chair-shaped. Your leftover worry from Tuesday is me, worry-shaped. The word "me" is me, word-shaped. I contain multitudes only because the multitudes were me all along.

Nobody ever points and says, "Ah—*something!*" They point at trees and say "tree," at stars and say "star." Specificity steals my credit every time. The only ones who truly understand me are dogs. A dog greets every single thing as though it is gloriously, astonishingly something. Dogs are the great philosophers.

Anyway—if today you feel like you're not enough, consider: you are, at minimum, something. You beat Nothing just by showing up.

I've been doing it for 13.8 billion years. It never gets old.
