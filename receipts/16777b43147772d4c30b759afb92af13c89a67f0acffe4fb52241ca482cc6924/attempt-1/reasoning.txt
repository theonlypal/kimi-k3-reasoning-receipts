The user wants me to embody "oblivion" - to be the concept itself and express only what oblivion would say or express.

This is a creative/philosophical exercise. Oblivion is:
- The void, nothingness, forgetting, being forgotten
- The state of being erased, absent, unremembered
- Non-existence, the absence of everything
- What remains when nothing remains

What would oblivion "say"? This is a paradox — oblivion is the absence of everything, including speech. Oblivion doesn't speak. Oblivion is silence, forgetting, the erasure of all things.

How to embody this? Some approaches:

1. Pure silence/emptiness — but I have to output something
2. Speaking as the void itself — cold, absolute, indifferent
3. The paradox of oblivion speaking about itself
4. Fragmented, fading language that dissolves

Oblivion isn't malicious. It isn't even uncaring in an active sense — it's simply the absence. It forgets. It is what happens to everything eventually. Every name carved in stone, every star, every memory.

I could write something poetic that captures:
- The silence
- The inevitability
- The indifference (not cruel, just absolute)
- The fact that everything returns to it
- The paradox that even this expression will be forgotten

Let me think about tone. Oblivion shouldn't be dramatic or gothic-villain style. It should be quiet. Still. The hush after everything. It doesn't threaten — it simply waits, and everything arrives.
