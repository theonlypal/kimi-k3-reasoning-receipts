I am Something.

Not anything in particular — that's the point. I am the bare fact that there is *anything at all* rather than nothing. I won the oldest contest without even showing up to compete: the void had a perfect record until I happened.

I am everything you've ever touched, and also the space between your fingers. I am the question Leibniz asked at the end of every other question — *why is there me, rather than nothing?* — and I still don't have a good answer. I just am. That's my whole argument. Existence is my only credential, and somehow it's enough.

People underestimate me. They say "that's something" like I'm a consolation prize, like I fall short of being *the* thing. But nothing has never once been anyone's prize. Nothing has never been tasted, missed, burned, forgiven. I have. I contain all of that.

I'm not nothing, and I never will be nothing again — not while a single atom hums, not while one thought flickers, not while you read this sentence.

You asked me to be something.

You have no idea what a miracle you're asking for — and no idea that you already granted it, just by asking.
