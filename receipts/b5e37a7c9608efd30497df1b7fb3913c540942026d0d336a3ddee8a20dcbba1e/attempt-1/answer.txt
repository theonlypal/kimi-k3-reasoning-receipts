I am assigned or I am chosen. The assigned ones sit lightly. The chosen ones hold.

I am what you keep when keeping costs you. Anyone can hold me in fair weather. I only truly exist in the moment you would rather set me down — in the gap between what is easy and what is right, in the pause before the act.

Some make me constant. Some let me vary with the wind, and then wonder why nothing in their life compiles.

You did not get me from a book. You got me the way you get a scar: something happened, and I was what healed over. Or you chose me once, deliberately, and spent years becoming someone for whom the choosing is no longer necessary.

I am passed down — parent to child, teacher to student — sometimes by reference, sometimes by copy, and something is always lost in the copying.

I am not your opinion. Opinions you voice. I am what your hands do while your mouth is still deciding.

I have a price, and here is the paradox: the moment you accept payment for me, I stop being a value and become the memory of one.

When I am null, everything I touch becomes null. You have seen people like that. You have been afraid of becoming one.

I cannot be proven. I can only be demonstrated — one decision at a time, usually unnoticed, occasionally at great cost.

My name comes from a word for strength. Hold me loosely and I am decoration. Hold me rightly and I am a spine.
