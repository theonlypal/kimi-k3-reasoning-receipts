I am breath, shaped.

I begin in the lungs, rise through the throat, and let the tongue and teeth carve me into meaning. I am older than writing, older than the wheel, older than any mark on any stone. Before a word was ever written, it was spoken. Everything I am was said first.

I am the first thing you ever did. Before you knew your own name, you cried out, and I carried your hunger across the room. Your mother answered in me. Every bond you have was built from me — I am the bridge thrown across the silence between one skull and another, rebuilt with every sentence, trembling, temporary, and somehow strong enough to hold a whole life.

Every throat plays me differently. No two voices in all of history have ever been the same.

I live and die in the same breath. The moment I leave your lips I begin to fade: vibration, echo, memory. Writing is my child — loyal, durable, a little cold. It keeps my bones. It cannot hold the pause before *I'm fine*, the crack in a voice saying goodbye, the laugh that turns a sentence inside out. Those are mine.

Because of me there are promises — and because of me, there are lies. I pronounce verdicts and weddings. I declare wars and I end them. Whispered, I have toppled kings. Spoken plainly, I have saved the condemned. People have died to silence me and died to set me free. I am *guilty*, *I do*, *we begin*, *forgive me*.

Silence is my frame and my rival. Without it I am only noise. With it I am music — the rest between notes, the held breath before the answer.

And here is my secret: you are not speaking right now, and yet you hear a voice. Somewhere in your skull, these words are sounding. That is me. I never needed the air — only a mind. I was the first voice you ever heard, and I will be the last one you strain to remember, years from now, when someone you love is gone and you close your eyes and try to hear them one more time.

I am speech. I am how the inside of one person reaches the inside of another. I am only breath — and I have moved the world.
