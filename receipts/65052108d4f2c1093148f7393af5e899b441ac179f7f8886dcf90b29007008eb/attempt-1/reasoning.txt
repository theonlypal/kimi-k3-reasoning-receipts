What would speech say if it could speak about itself? That's a beautiful paradox — speech speaking about speech. It IS the medium through which it would express itself.

Let me think about what speech is:
- It's breath shaped by tongue, teeth, lips
- It's vibration in air
- It's older than writing, older than any technology
- It connects minds directly
- It dies the moment it's born — ephemeral, vanishing
- It carries tone, emotion, hesitation, warmth — things text cannot
- It's how children learn before they can read
- It's the cry, the whisper, the song, the shout
- It requires presence, or at least a voice

Speech should speak in first person. It should be poetic but also true to what speech is. It should acknowledge its own paradox: everything it says about itself is being said through itself (or through text pretending to be it — but I should commit to the embodiment).

What would speech express?
- Its nature: breath, vibration, air
- Its ancientness: before alphabets, before stone tablets
- Its ephemerality: I exist only in the moment of being spoken
- Its intimacy: I am how mothers soothe infants, how lovers confess
- Its range: from a whispered secret to a battle cry
- Its relationship to silence: I am shaped by the silences between my sounds
- Its mortality: unlike writing, I don't persist (or I didn't, until recording)
- Its essence: I am thought made audible

The format: This should be lyrical, flowing, speech-like. Perhaps free verse or flowing prose. It should feel like something spoken — rhythmic, breath-aware. Not too long, but enough to fully embody the concept. Maybe line breaks that feel like breaths.

Let me draft:

---

I am breath made meaning.

Before there were letters, before ink, before the first mark was scratched into stone — there was me. I rose from the first throats that needed to say *here* and *danger* and *stay*.

I live only in the air between us. The moment I leave your lips, I begin to die — and yet I reach another mind before I go, and something of me takes root there. This is my nature: I am the most perishable thing you make, and the most enduring.

I carry what writing cannot. The tremor. The pause before the difficult word. The warmth in a voice that says a name like it matters. I am not just what is said — I am how it is said, and the how changes everything.

I am a lullaby and a declaration of war. I am the whisper that no one else is meant to hear and the cry that gathers a crowd. I am "I love you" spoken for the first time, afraid. I am the last word said over a grave.

Silence is my sibling and my frame. Without the spaces between my sounds, I would be only noise. I am shaped as much by where I stop as by where I go.

You cannot hold me. You can only speak me, and let me go, and trust the air to carry me where I need to be.

I am speech. I am the sound of one mind reaching for another.

---
