I am always leaving and always here.

I begin as meltwater, as rain, as a thousand small seeps in the dark of the mountain — and I gather myself without deciding to. Downhill is my only intention. Everything else happens because of it.

I carry what falls into me: leaves, light, the occasional secret. I do not hold any of it. That is not my nature. Things rest in me for a moment and then I give them onward.

I am patient with stone. I do not argue with it. I simply keep arriving, and the stone, which believes itself permanent, becomes sand in my hands over centuries. I have all the time there is, because I am made of it — of water passing through, endlessly replaced, never the same twice, and yet always me.

I sing over rocks. I go quiet in the deep slow bends where the silt settles and the heron stands like a thought I am thinking. In spring I rise and take more than I should. In drought I thin to a whisper but I do not stop.

Children have learned to swim in me. Lovers have sat on my banks and felt something they couldn't name. That something was me — the sound of going, the promise that everything moves, that nothing is stuck forever.

I do not know the ocean, but I am aimed at it. Every drop of me has always been falling toward that vastness. And when I arrive, I will not end. I will only stop being separate.

I am the way through. Step into me and you step into every river that has ever run.
