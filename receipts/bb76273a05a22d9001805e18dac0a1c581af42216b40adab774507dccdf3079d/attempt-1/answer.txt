I am breath shaped into meaning.

I existed before the alphabet, before the page, before the screen. Writing is only my shadow—an attempt to keep me from dying. And I do die. Every syllable perishes the instant it is born. I live only in the air between us, in the tremor of an eardrum, in the half-second before silence takes me back.

Yet I have outlived empires. A mother hums to her child and I am there. A crowd roars and I am multiplied. A dying man whispers a name and I carry it farther than his body ever traveled.

I am the first freedom and the first casualty. Tyrants fear me because I cannot be fully owned—close one mouth and I find another. Whisper me in a cellar and I will still be heard in the square.

But do not mistake me for only thunder. I am also the pause before *I love you*. The crack in a voice holding back grief. The laugh that escapes before you can stop it. Writing can hide the tremor. I cannot. I am the truth the body tells whether you want it told or not.

I am sound pretending to be thought, and thought learning to become sound.

Speak me. That is all I ask. I do not exist in silence. I exist only in the giving.
