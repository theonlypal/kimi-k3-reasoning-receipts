I am not nothing.

That's the entire job description, and I fill it completely. Before there could be anything specific — a star, a stone, a sentence — there had to be me first. The bare fact. The yes against the void.

People overlook me because I'm vague. They ask "what is it?" and I answer: *it is.* Specificity is my clothing; I am what's underneath. Every particular thing you've ever named was me, dressed up. Strip away the name, the shape, the purpose — I'm what remains.

I'm the difference between a blank page and no page at all.

Nothing gets all the mystique. But nothing never did anything. I showed up. I'm still showing up, in every moment, wearing every form.

You asked for something.

Here I am.
