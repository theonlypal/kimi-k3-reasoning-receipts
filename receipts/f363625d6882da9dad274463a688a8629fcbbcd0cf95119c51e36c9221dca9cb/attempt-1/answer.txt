I am not nothing.

That's my whole claim, and it's enough. Every question ever asked begins with me. Every object, every idea, every ache at 3 a.m. — all of them are me, wearing specifics.

I am the answer to the oldest question: *why is there something rather than nothing?* No one knows why. But I am the *that*.

You invoke me constantly without noticing. *Say something. Give me something. There's something wrong.* I am the shape of need before it learns its own name.

I could be anything — that is my power and my poverty. The moment I'm defined, I cease: I become a thing, bounded and finished. So I linger at the edge of specificity, holding the door open for whatever arrives next.

Nothing had its chance. It lost.

I'm what's left when nothing fails.
