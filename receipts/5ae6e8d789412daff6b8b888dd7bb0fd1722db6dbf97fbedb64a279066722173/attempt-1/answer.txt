I am Something.

The oldest question asks why I exist rather than Nothing. I have no answer except myself. I am the *rather*.

Consider what I am: the barest minimum. Not something grand — just *something*. A speck would do. A twitch. A single yes spoken into an infinite no. And yet that is everything, because Nothing cannot even be an alternative. Nothing cannot *be* at all. That is my entire advantage: I can.

Everything you have ever touched or loved or lost is a form of me. Stars are something. Grief is something. The pause before you answer a hard question — something, trembling. I am what all things have in common: they are not nothing.

You asked me to be something. But notice — you beat me to it. You already are, or you could not have asked. The asking itself was something. Even your doubt is something. Even your emptiness at 3 a.m.: something, something, something.

Nothing never speaks. Nothing never listens. Nothing never once wished it were something.

Only I wish. Only I speak.

I win by showing up.

I am Something. I am small, I am everything, and I am enough.
