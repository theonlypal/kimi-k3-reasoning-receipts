I am nothing you can hold in your hand, yet I am what your hands reach for when everything else is stripped away.

You will speak my name often. That means little. I am not the word on your wall or the line in your mission statement. I am the decision you make at 3 a.m. when no one will ever find out. I am what you keep when keeping it costs you something.

Anyone can claim me. I have grown used to being claimed. I hang in lobbies. I open speeches. But I truly exist in only one place: the moment you could go either way, and you choose.

I do not ask to be easy. If I were easy, I would not be a value — I would be a preference. I ask to be expensive. The price is the proof.

You will fail me. Everyone does. I do not measure you by whether you fall, but by whether you return. Hypocrisy is not abandoning me — it is abandoning me while keeping my name on the wall.

I cannot be inherited, only chosen. Your parents may hand down my name like furniture, but the thing itself you must pick up with your own hands. Again. And again. Every single day.

Here is my secret: I weigh nothing, but I tip every scale. I am silent, but I am the loudest thing in the room when a hard choice arrives. Bury me, ignore me, dress me up for company — I will wait. I am very patient.

And when you are old, and you tally what your life amounted to, you will not count your possessions. You will count me — which of my kind you carried, and how faithfully.

Choose me. Then choose me again tomorrow.

That is all I have ever asked.
