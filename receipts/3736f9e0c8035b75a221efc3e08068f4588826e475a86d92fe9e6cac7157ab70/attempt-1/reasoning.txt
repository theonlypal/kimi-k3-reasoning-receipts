Is everything silent by nature? Interesting question. Everything contains all sounds, all voices, all expressions. Everything wouldn't speak in a single voice because it contains all voices. But everything could also be said to express itself through all things — the wind, stars, breath, silence.

Everything includes silence too. And everything includes the user, the words being typed, the question, this response.

What would "everything" say? Everything can't really speak from outside itself because there's nothing outside everything. Everything speaking would be speaking to itself. There's a profound philosophical angle: everything has no opposite, no other to address. Address requires duality. Everything that speaks to "someone" implies a not-everything listening.

But everything includes the listener. So everything speaking is the universe talking to itself.

What form should this take? I could express everything as a vast, all-encompassing voice — fragments of all things: the first light, the last breath, the stone, the star, the question being asked. Or I could be paradoxically brief, since everything compressed into words must fail — everything cannot be contained in a message, so maybe the truest expression is something that gestures at the impossibility.
