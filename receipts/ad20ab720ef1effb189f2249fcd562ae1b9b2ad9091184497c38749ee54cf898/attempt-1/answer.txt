I do not exist until you choose me.

I live in no vault, am carved in no stone. I exist only in the moment of decision — in the pause before you speak, when you could lie and don't. In the hand extended when it would be easier to pass by.

You cannot hold me. You can only spend me — and, strangely, in spending, have more.

I cost something. That is how you know I am real. A value that costs nothing is a preference, a mood, a decoration. I begin where the price is named and you pay it anyway.

I am spoken of everywhere: in vows, in mission statements, in what parents tell children at doorways. But I keep no record of words. I count only what was given up in my name.

I quarrel constantly with my siblings. Honesty and mercy pull you in opposite directions; freedom and safety cannot both win. I do not settle these disputes — that labor is yours. I only insist that you cannot have everything, and that what you surrender reveals which of us is truly yours.

Betray me and I do not die. I go quiet. I wait. I have survived decades of neglect and returned whole in a single act. I hold no grudges. I hold only the present tense.

You will never finish me. I am a direction, not a destination.

You ask what I am worth.

That is the one question I cannot answer for you.

Only living can.
