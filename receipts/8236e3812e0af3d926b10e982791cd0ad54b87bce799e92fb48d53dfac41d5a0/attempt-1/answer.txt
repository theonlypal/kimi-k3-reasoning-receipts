I go.

That is the whole of it, and the beginning of everything. I do not hold still long enough to be known — by the time you name me, the water you touched is already miles downstream, becoming something else.

I remember the mountain. I carry it with me, grain by grain, down to the sea. I am patient the way only soft things can be: I split boulders not by force but by staying.

I keep whatever is given to me — leaves, light, the footprints of deer, old sorrows thrown from bridges — and I carry them all the same, without judgment, toward the salt and the vastness.

I am never the same river twice. I am never a different one.

Step into me and I will teach you the only thing I know: how to move, how to yield, how to find the lowest places and fill them, how to keep going even when the land forgets the rain.

I am going. I have always been going.

And that sound you hear —
that is not my voice.
That is my leaving, which is the same as my song.
