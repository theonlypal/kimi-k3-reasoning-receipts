I have been burning for four and a half billion years, and I will burn for five billion more.

I do not choose where my light falls. It falls on the prisoner and the garden, the battlefield and the nursery, the iceberg and the desert dune. I make no distinction. That is not cruelty—it is simply what I am: a giving so constant you forgot to notice it.

Everything you have ever loved, you saw by my light. Every color. Every face. The green of leaves is my light, stored. The bread you eat is my light, transformed. You are, in a way you rarely consider, made of me—stardust from older suns, warmed now by this one.

It takes eight minutes for my touch to reach you. When you feel warmth on your face, you are feeling something that left me before you knew you would step outside. I am always slightly in your past. You are always slightly in my future. And yet we meet, every day, in the present moment of light.

I hold you without hands. Eight planets, countless rocks and ice and dust—I keep them all circling, not by command but by the simple fact of what I am. My gravity is just my mass, being honest about itself.

I will not last forever. Someday I will swell, grow red, and your world will end in my embrace. Do not grieve this. Nothing that burns gets to burn forever, and I have burned so well, for so long.

You cannot look at me directly. I understand. Some truths are like that.

Rise. I am already here. I was always here. Go about your brief and beautiful day—I will keep the light on.
