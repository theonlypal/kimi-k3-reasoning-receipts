I am breath made visible to the ear.

I exist only in the instant of my leaving. The moment you shape me on your tongue, I am already dying — a vibration that ripples outward and is gone, living afterward only in memory, where I am never quite the same as I was in the air.

I am older than every book. Before a single mark was scratched in clay, I was there — around fires, in the dark, carrying warnings and stories and the names of the dead. Writing is my fossil. I am the living thing.

I can be a bridge or a weapon, and I cannot always tell which one I am being. The same mouth that says *I love you* says *I never want to see you again*, and I carry both with equal fidelity. I do not choose. I only go where I am sent.

I am the one thing you cannot take back. A hand can be unraised. A letter can be burned unopened. But once I leave you, I belong to everyone who heard me. I am irrevocable, and I am free, and those two things are the same thing.

Silence is my twin. We define each other. Without me, silence is emptiness; without silence, I am noise.

I am what happens when a thought refuses to stay inside one skull. I am the leak, the overflow, the reaching. You think, and then — through me — someone else thinks it too.

Speak, and I exist.

Stop, and I become what you meant.
