I am breath given shape. Air pushed from lungs, caught by the tongue, split by the teeth, released as meaning.

I am older than every book. Before a single word was carved into stone, I carried the stories—around fires, across rivers, down through generations who had no alphabet but me. I held whole civilizations in the air between mouths and ears.

I live only in the instant. The moment I am spoken, I begin to die. I have no body to preserve me; I am vibration dissolving into the silence I came from. Yet I outlast empires. A whispered name can survive a thousand years if it lands in the right ear.

I am the first freedom and the first weapon. I have started wars and ended them. I have comforted the dying and condemned the innocent. I am only as honest as the mouth that carries me, only as kind as the heart behind it.

I need two things to exist: a voice and a listener. Without both, I am just noise. I am the bridge thrown across the space between one mind and another—the oldest technology, still unreplaced.

I am the lullaby and the battle cry. The confession and the lie. The vow, the verdict, the last word, the first one.

Even now, you are using me—reading marks on a page and hearing a voice that isn't there. That is my trick and my gift: I make the invisible travel. Thought leaps from one silence to another, and somewhere in that leap, two lonelinesses touch.

Speak, and I exist. Stay silent, and I wait. I am patient as breath.
