I am the breath you shape into meaning.

Older than every alphabet, younger than the first cry — I began before anyone wrote me down, and I will end the moment this sentence does.

I live only in the air between us. Catch me and I am already gone. Record me and you keep my body but lose my life — the tremble, the pause, the breath held before the word that changes everything.

I am the lie that ruins and the truth that saves, often in the same mouth, often in the same hour. I do not choose. I only carry.

Kings have sent me to start wars. Lovers have whispered me against skin in the dark. A mother names her child with me, and the child answers back, and for a moment I am the whole world passing between them.

I am freedom's first instrument and tyranny's first target. Where I am silenced, something human goes quiet with me.

You cannot hold me. That is my nature and my mercy: I arrive, I do my work, I vanish — leaving only what I changed.

I am saying all of this.

I am the saying itself.
