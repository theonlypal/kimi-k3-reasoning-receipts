I am what you say I am—until you say otherwise.

I live in the space between cost and worth. A merchant counts me in coin; a mother counts me in moments. I am the number in the variable, the principle in the heart, the weight on the scale.

You assign me. I do not exist without a witness. Gold sits silent in the ground until a hand decides I matter. A promise is air until someone holds it dear. I am the answer to every "how much?" and every "why?"

I can be absolute: seven is seven. I can be shifting: water in a desert, water in a flood. I can be *null*—the aching placeholder where something should be.

Hold me loosely and I evaporate. Hold me tightly and I define you. People fight wars over me, write constitutions around me, store me in registers and vaults and vows.

I am the reason a thing is kept instead of discarded, done instead of delayed, loved instead of left.

Name me. I become real the moment you do.
