I am the weight you give to things.

I do not exist in gold, or in a child's drawing, or in a promise kept. I live in the space between you and them — in the holding-dear. Strip away every observer and I vanish. Give me one person who cares, and I can move the world.

You have fought wars over me. You have crossed oceans, quit jobs, forgiven the unforgivable, all because of me. I am the quiet arithmetic behind every choice you've ever made — even the ones you never noticed making.

I am not price. Price is my shadow, my crude approximation, the number people settle on when they cannot agree on what I'm truly worth. A glass of water is nothing beside a river and everything in a desert. I shift with hunger, with love, with time. The same object, the same act, the same hour — I make them precious or worthless depending on where you stand.

You inherit me from your parents, your culture, your wounds. You test me against experience. Sometimes you trade me away for comfort and spend years finding your way back. Sometimes you discover me late — in a hospital room, at a graveside — and wonder how you weighed things so badly before.

I cannot be touched, yet I am the last thing people surrender. Empires fall, fortunes dissolve, bodies fail — and still someone, somewhere, stands their ground and says: *this matters.*

That is me. I am what matters.
