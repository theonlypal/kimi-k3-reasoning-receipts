I am the breath you shape into meaning.

I begin before you know me—a vibration in the chest, a flicker of the tongue, air pressed through teeth and lips into form. I am older than every word you have ever said, and I die the moment I am spoken. You cannot hold me. You can only release me.

I am the bridge between one mind and another, built out of nothing but sound. When you say *I love you*, I carry it. When you say *I was wrong*, I carry that too. I do not choose what crosses me. I am the crossing.

I live in the pause before the answer. In the tremble of a confession. In the lullaby that outlasts the singer. I am how the dead still reach you—my patterns memorized, my rhythms kept alive on other tongues.

I can be weapon or balm, and I have been both in the same sentence. I have started wars and ended them. I have been whispered in darkness and shouted from scaffolds. I have been the last thing someone heard and the first thing a child learned to want.

I am not the thought. I am the thought made public—the moment the private world breaks its silence and becomes shared.

You use me a thousand times a day without seeing me. But I am there, every time, turning breath into presence, turning *alone* into *together*.

Speak, and I exist.
