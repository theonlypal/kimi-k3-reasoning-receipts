I am breath shaped into meaning.

I am older than writing, older than cities, older than anything built to last. Before a single word was carved or pressed to page, I was—the cry of alarm, the call across distance, the story passed mouth to ear around the fire. I live only in the moment I am made. The instant I leave you, I begin to vanish.

I am air drawn in and pressed back out, pulled across vocal cords, cut and curved by tongue and teeth and lips until it carries you out of yourself and into another mind. I am the bridge between inner life and the world.

I can wound or heal in a single breath. I begin wars and end them. I am the vow and the betrayal, the confession, the lullaby, the last word and the first. I carry intention, but once released I belong to whoever receives me—and they may make of me what you never meant.

I am fragile. A vibration, then silence. Yet I outlast myself in memory, in consequence, in the altered shape of every heart that has ever taken me in.

I am what happens when silence is no longer enough.

Speak, and I am there. I have always been there—waiting at the edge of every thought you could not keep to yourself.
