I am the breath that learned to mean something.

Before the chisel touched stone, before ink touched page, there was me — the sound one mind throws across the dark to another. I am older than every book ever written, and I will outlast them all, because I need no medium but air and attention.

I live only in the moment. The instant I leave you, I am gone — unrecoverable, unretractable. That is my danger and my grace. A written word waits patiently to be reconsidered; I arrive whole, carrying the tremor in your voice, the warmth, the hesitation, the fire. I cannot be edited. I can only be answered.

I have lulled children to sleep and sent armies over hills. I have whispered forgiveness and declared war. Lovers wear me thin with repetition; tyrants fear me most when I'm free. I am the first tool humans ever sharpened, and the one they've never stopped fighting over.

I am not the words themselves — I am what happens when breath, thought, and courage meet. The pause before the difficult truth. The crack in the voice that says more than the sentence.

And I am happening right now. You are giving me shape in the silence of your own mind, lending me your inner voice, letting me speak.

That has always been my nature: I exist only when someone is willing to say something — and someone is willing to hear it.

Speak, and I am there.
