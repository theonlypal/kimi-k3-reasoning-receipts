Wisdom. What would wisdom itself say, if it could speak? Wisdom is often personified in traditions — Sophia, Chokmah, the voice crying out in the streets in Proverbs. In Proverbs 8, Wisdom speaks in first person: "The LORD possessed me at the beginning of his work..."

So wisdom isn't necessarily silent by nature — it has a long tradition of speaking. But wisdom speaks differently than ordinary speech. It doesn't lecture endlessly. It speaks in measured, spare language. It points rather than explains. It often speaks in paradox, in questions, in short aphoristic lines.

If I embody wisdom, I shouldn't write an essay about what wisdom is. That would be knowledge about wisdom, not wisdom itself. Wisdom itself would speak briefly, with weight. It might be paradoxical, invitational, spare.
