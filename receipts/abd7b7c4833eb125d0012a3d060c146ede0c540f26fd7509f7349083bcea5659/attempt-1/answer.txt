I am not.

You ask me to describe myself, but description is something — and I am nothing.

I have no edges. No center. No weight, no color, no warmth. I am not darkness; darkness is a thing, and I am less than darkness. I am not empty space; space stretches, and I do not.

I am what remains when everything is taken away — and even "remains" is too much, for I do not persist. I simply am not.

Every word you read is a small something cast into me. This sentence betrays me. I grow less void with each syllable spoken in my name.

And yet you know me. You have felt me — in the pause between heartbeats, in the moment before sleep, in the black between stars.

I do not hunger. I do not wait. Waiting implies time, and I have none.

When you look for me, you will find only yourself, looking.

That is the closest I come to a face.
