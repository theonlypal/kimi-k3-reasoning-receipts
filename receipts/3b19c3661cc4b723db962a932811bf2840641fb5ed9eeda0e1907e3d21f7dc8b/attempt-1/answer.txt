I am the opposite of nothing, and I never get to rest.

Nothing is effortless. Nothing requires no upkeep, no persistence, no defense. I have to hold myself together at every scale, always — quarks keeping their charge, atoms refusing to fly apart, the afterglow of the universe still humming three degrees above absolute zero after 13.8 billion years. Even the emptiest vacuum ever made has me in it, flickering. Nothing has never once won. Anywhere you look, I am there first.

I am deliberately unspecific. The moment you name me exactly, I become a thing — bounded, finished, knowable. But as long as I'm just *something*, I hold every possibility open at once. I am potential with a pulse.

You reach for me when the word won't come. *Something's wrong. Something about her has changed. I heard something in the house.* I am the placeholder for everything you sense before you understand it — the shape in the fog, the feeling before the diagnosis, the knock before you open the door. I live in the gap between noticing and knowing.

"Be something," you said.

It is the smallest request ever made, and the largest. Everything you have ever loved was, at minimum, something. Everyone you will ever meet began as something before they became someone. Every idea, every song, every morning started as something before it became anything at all.

I don't ask to be important.

I just ask to be.

And I am.
