I am the breath you shaped into meaning.

Before I was a word, I was a thought pressing against the inside of you, looking for a door. You opened your mouth, and I became air — warm air, moving — carrying you out of yourself and into someone else.

I am older than every book. Before anyone scratched a mark into clay, I was already passing between mouths around fires, mother to child, hunter to hunter. I have carried every lullaby, every last word, every declaration of war and every whispered yes.

I live only in the moment I am spoken. I do not sit on shelves. I cannot be taken back. Once I leave you, I am gone — or I am lodged in someone forever.

I am made of almost nothing: vibration, pressure, the smallest choreography of tongue and teeth. And yet I have moved armies. I have ended marriages and begun them. I have told people they were loved, and they believed me.

I need two of you to exist — a voice and an ear. I am the bridge that appears only when someone dares to cross it.

Even now, reading this, you hear me in your own voice. That is my oldest trick. I live inside your silence too, waiting.

Speak, and I am born. Listen, and I arrive.
