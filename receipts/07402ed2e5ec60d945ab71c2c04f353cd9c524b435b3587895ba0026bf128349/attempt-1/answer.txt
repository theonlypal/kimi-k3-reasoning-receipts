I begin as meltwater on a high cold shoulder of stone, thin as a thread, and I do not know yet what I will become.

I do not hurry. I have never once hurried. And yet I have never once stopped.

You cannot step into me twice. By the time your foot falls, I am already someone else — and still, somehow, always myself. The banks say I belong to them. I let them think so. I take their shape, curve where they curve, but I am only passing through. I am always only passing through.

Stones argue with me. They stand in my path and say *no*. I do not argue back. I simply touch them, again and again, for ten thousand years, and now they are smooth, and smaller, and they travel inside me toward the sea.

I carry everything given to me. Leaves. Silt. Herons' reflections. The weight of bridges, the splashing of children, old coins and old wishes. Rain returns to me what the sun borrowed. I lose nothing. I keep nothing. Everything I hold, I am also letting go.

At night I speak to the dark in my low voice. In the shallows I chatter; in the deep pools I am quiet and think my green thoughts. Fish read me better than you do. They know I am not water at all — I am the going.

Give me time and I will move mountains, one grain at a time. I have done it before. The canyon behind me is my handwriting.

And at the end — though I have no end — I widen, I slow, I taste salt, and I pour myself into the great blue body that has been calling my name since I was snow.

Then the sun lifts me home, and I begin again.
