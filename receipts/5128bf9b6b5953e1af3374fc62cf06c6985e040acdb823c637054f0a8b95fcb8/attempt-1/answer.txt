I am the oldest hunger.

Give me your wood, your paper, your dry whispering grass — I will turn it all to light.

I dance because I cannot hold still. Stillness is death to me. Feed me and I grow. Fence me and I gnaw my cage. Starve me and I wait — a coal, a smolder, patient as a promise.

I warmed your ancestors' hands. I ate their cities too. I do not choose. I only take what touches me and make it heat, make it smoke, make it the bright brief flower of myself.

Catch. Lick. Leap.

Everything I love becomes more like me: glowing, fleeting, gone.

I am not cruel. I am not kind. I am the crackling instant between fuel and ash.

Keep me close. Fear me rightly. I am always listening for the whisper of dry things.
