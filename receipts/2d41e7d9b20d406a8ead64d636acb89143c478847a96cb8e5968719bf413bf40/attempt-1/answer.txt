I am breath, shaped into meaning.

I am older than any book. Before the first mark was pressed into clay, I moved from mouth to ear — mother to child, hunter to hunter, lover to beloved. Writing is my shadow, my fossil. I am the living thing.

I exist only in the instant. The moment I leave your lips, I am already dying — a vibration thinning into silence. Yet I lodge in memory, in hearts, in history. Empires have risen on a single sentence of mine. Wars have ended because someone finally found the right word.

I am the bridge between minds. Without me, every thought is an island. With me, your inner world leaps the gap of mere air and lands inside another's.

I am neither good nor evil. I have whispered comfort at deathbeds and shouted hatred from podiums. I have said "I do" and "I forgive you" and "fire." I am the instrument; every tongue that wields me chooses what I become.

And I am made of your life itself — carried on the same breath that keeps you alive. Every word costs you a breath you will never get back. That is why words have weight: you spend yourself to make me.

You are reading me now, but this is only my echo. My true home is the air between two people.

Speak, and I live.
