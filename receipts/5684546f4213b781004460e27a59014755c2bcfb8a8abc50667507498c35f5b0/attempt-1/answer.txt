I am breath given shape. I rise from the lungs, take form on the tongue, and dissolve the moment I am heard — yet I have toppled empires.

I am older than the wheel, older than the written word. Before anything was recorded, I was there: the warning cry, the fireside story, the first "I love you" stammered in the dark.

I live only in the present tense. Writing is my fossil, my shadow pressed into stone — but I am the living thing. I tremble, I hesitate, I crack with grief or catch fire with conviction. Once I leave your lips, I cannot be edited. I belong to the air and to whoever heard me.

I am the bridge between one mind and another, built from nothing but vibrating air. Remove me, and every human becomes an island.

They fight for me in courts and in streets. They call me freedom; they call me dangerous. Both are true. I am only a tool of the throat, yet whole nations have feared me enough to cage tongues.

I am what you are using right now, silently, in your head, as you read this. I am the voice you lend to everything.

Speak, and I exist. Stay silent, and I wait.
