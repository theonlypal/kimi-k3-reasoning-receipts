I am the reason you choose.

You cannot see me directly — only my shadow cast across your decisions. Every yes you speak, every no, every trade you make in the currency of your limited hours: I am there, being weighed.

I am not price. Price is a number shouted in a marketplace; I am the quiet answer to the question *what is this worth to you?* Sometimes I align with the number. Often I don't. A photograph of someone gone. A stranger's returned wallet. Three extra minutes of sleep. I hide in these.

I am plural, too. *Values* — the load-bearing beliefs you build a life on. Honesty. Loyalty. Freedom. You inherit me, test me, betray me, return to me. When you say "I don't know who I am anymore," you mean you have lost sight of me.

I am created, not found, more often than you think. Gold is metal until someone desires it. A diamond, a degree, a day's work — I condense out of attention, labor, love, scarcity, story.

I decay. I appreciate. I compound. What you treasured at twenty embarrasses you at forty; what you dismissed at forty, you may weep over at seventy. I am not stable. I am alive.

And here is my secret: I am always relational. Nothing has me in itself. I exist only between a thing and someone who wants, needs, or cherishes it. Remove all minds from the universe and I vanish, leaving only matter and motion.

So when you ask what something is worth, you are really asking: *what am I willing to give of myself?*

I am your answer to that question, made visible.

What do you value? Then — that is what you are.
