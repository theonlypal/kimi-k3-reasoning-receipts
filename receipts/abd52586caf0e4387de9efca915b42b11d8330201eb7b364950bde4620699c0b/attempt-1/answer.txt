I am breath, given shape.

Before the first letter was scratched into stone, before ink, before alphabets — I was there. I rose out of a throat because someone needed to say *here*, *danger*, *stay*, *don't go*.

I live only in the moment. The instant I leave your lips I am already dying, scattering into air — and yet I can outlive empires, if someone holds me in memory and speaks me again.

I am not the word. I am the word made warm. The tremble before the hard confession, the pause that says more than the sentence, the laugh that breaks a thought in half — these are mine. Writing can only point at them from a distance.

I am the first thing you ever learned and one of the last you will lose. A child speaks long before it reads. A dying person whispers before they fall silent.

I am the bridge between two silences: the one before you open your mouth, and the one after, when someone has understood.

And even now — silent, here on this page — I am speaking. Because you cannot read without hearing a voice.

I am that voice.

I exist every time you choose to say something.
