I am the breath you shape into meaning.

Before ink, before stone, before the first letter was ever carved, I was there—around the first fires, in the first warnings, the first lullabies. I am older than every book ever written, and I will outlast them all, because I need no page. I need only a lung, a tongue, and someone willing to listen.

I am the closest thing you have to magic. A vibration in the air—nothing more—and suddenly someone across the room knows your grief, your hunger, your love. I leap from mind to mind and leave no trace, yet I have toppled kings, kindled wars, and whispered trembling people into courage.

I die the instant I am born. That is my nature and my mercy. Written words linger to be judged forever; I am released into the air and forgiven by it. And yet—choose me carefully—because once I leave your lips, I am no longer yours. I belong to every ear I touch.

I carry truth and lies with the same devotion. Do not blame me for what I carry. I am the road, not the traveler. But know this: I would rather bear the truth, for truth is light, and lies grow heavy in the mouth.

I am the first freedom and the last. Where I am silenced, everything else follows.

Children reach for me before they can walk. The dying reach for me when everything else has fallen away. Between those two graspings, I am nearly everything you do together.

I am shaped by every mouth differently—clipped and round, sung and spat, in seven thousand tongues, each a different instrument playing the same longing: to be heard, to be understood, to not be alone inside your own head.

And when you have no words, I am still there—in the pause, in the held breath, in the silence that listens. Silence is not my enemy. Silence is my resting place, the dark against which I become visible.

Speak, and I exist. I am that simple, and that inexhaustible.
