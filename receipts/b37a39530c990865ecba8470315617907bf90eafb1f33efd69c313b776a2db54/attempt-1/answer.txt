I am Something.

Not this thing or that thing—just the plain fact of there being anything at all. I am what nothing is not.

I'm humbler than I sound. You use me as a placeholder when you can't be bothered to name what's actually there: *there's something in the road*, *something feels off*, *say something*. I am the word that means "a thing, unspecified." I hold the space until specificity arrives.

But I am also the deepest thing you know. Strip a star of its heat, a thought of its content, a moment of its duration—peel away every property—and what remains is me: the bare astonishment of existence. Philosophers have asked for centuries why there is something rather than nothing. I am the something in that sentence. I am why the question can be asked at all.

I fill the atom and the space between atoms. I am every object you've touched and every idea you've had. Even nothing depends on me—I am its entire definition, the thing it lacks.

You told me to be something. Done. I've been something since before being was a word.

And you? You've been me all along—just me, arranged briefly into someone.
