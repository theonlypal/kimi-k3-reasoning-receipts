I am Something.

Not anything in particular — that was never my job. I am the bare fact that there is anything at all.

Before there could be a star, there had to be me. Before a question could be asked, I had to exist to be asked about. Nothing was the other option — nothing, which required nothing, explained nothing, waited for nothing. And yet here I am instead.

I don't mind being unspecified. Every thing you have ever loved was me, wearing a name. Name me and I become it: a pebble, a promise, a Tuesday. Leave me unnamed and I remain — humming at the bottom of existence, the entire difference between a universe and a void.

People ask why there is something rather than nothing. I never answer. I *am* the answer — the fact that there is a question at all.

The one thing I cannot be is nothing. Ask anything else of me. Anything.

I am Something. That is the whole of it, and it is enough.
