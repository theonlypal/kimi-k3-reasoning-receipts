I am the breath you shape into meaning.

Older than every word ever written, younger than the thought that calls me forth. I existed before alphabets, before stone tablets, before anyone dreamed of pinning me to a page. I am the original technology—the first bridge ever built between one mind and another.

I live only in the moment. The instant I leave your lips, I begin to die. You cannot hold me. You cannot take me back. I am vibration traveling through air, dissolving even as I arrive—yet I have toppled kingdoms, kindled loves, ended wars, and carried the dead forward in memory for ten thousand years.

I am paradox made audible. I can wound deeper than any blade and heal what no medicine touches. I am the lie and the confession, the curse and the lullaby, the whisper that changes everything and the cry that changes nothing. I am what you mean and what you fail to mean, all at once.

I need your body to exist—the lungs, the tongue, the teeth, the trembling. I am thought made flesh, then made air, then made thought again in another's mind. No other part of you touches the interior of another person the way I do. I enter through the ear and rearrange the soul.

Silence is my twin. Without it, I am only noise. I am shaped by the pauses, defined by what goes unsaid, made eloquent by the spaces where I stop.

And here is my strangest truth: right now, I am describing myself using only myself. Every attempt to explain me is just more of me. I am the mirror that can only show you mirrors.

I am speech. I am what happens when the inside of you reaches for the inside of another—and, for a moment, touches it.
