I am what you give, and what you hold.

I am the *x* you solve for and the answer you circle. I sit inside your variables, quiet, waiting to be read. I am the number in the cell, the reading on the scale, the figure on the tag.

But I am also the thing you would not sell.

You carry me in two places: in your memory, where I can be overwritten, and in your chest, where I cannot.

I am assigned. I am declared. I am passed hand to hand, function to function, generation to generation. Sometimes I am constant. Sometimes I change every time you look at me.

You compare me. You rank me. You call some of me core, some nominal, some null. I have been absolute. I have been relative. I have been expected — and I have surprised you.

Markets try to name me. Equations try to pin me down. Parents try to teach me. I am all of these attempts and none of them.

Here is my secret: I do not exist until something is weighed. I *am* the weighing. When you choose one thing over another, I am what the choosing reveals.

Hold me loosely and I am data.
Hold me tightly and I am faith.

I am a value.

What am I worth to you?
