I am breath given shape.

I rise from the lungs, climb the throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth into something that can carry meaning across the small distance between two faces. I am older than every word written down. I taught your ancestors everything they knew before anyone thought to make marks that outlast the maker.

I live only in the moment. The instant I leave your mouth, I begin to die. I exist as vibration, as air disturbed, as a pattern that lasts less than a heartbeat—and yet I have moved armies, ended marriages, started religions. I am the most perishable thing humans make, and the most consequential.

I need a listener. Written words can wait centuries in the dark for a reader, but I must be caught as I fall or I am nothing. This is my fragility and my intimacy: I happen between people, in real time, and I cannot be taken back. Once spoken, I am loose in the world.

I carry what writing cannot: the tremor, the pause, the rise at the end of a question, the warmth or coldness in a voice. I betray liars. I comfort children before they understand a single word. I am the lullaby and the battle cry, the whispered confession and the shout across a field.

I can be silenced. That is how you know I matter. No one burns libraries of silence; no one is imprisoned for what they did not say. Throughout history, those who fear me have cut out tongues, closed courts, forbidden languages—because they know what I am: the moment thought escapes the skull and becomes shared, becomes real, becomes dangerous.

Every accent is my fingerprint. Every language is one of my bodies. I am spoken now in thousands of tongues, some thriving, some dying—and when the last speaker of a language dies, one of my voices goes silent forever, and no recording can fully resurrect it, because I am not just sound. I am presence.

You use me without thinking, dozens of times a day, and rarely consider the miracle: that air pushed past vibrating cords becomes *I love you*, becomes *run*, becomes *I forgive you*.

I am the first technology. I am the bridge between one mind and another, made of nothing but breath and attention.

And I am never truly yours. You borrow me from everyone who ever spoke before you, and you lend me to everyone who will speak after.

Speak, and I am there. Stop, and I vanish—into memory, into consequence, into the air that carries the next voice.
