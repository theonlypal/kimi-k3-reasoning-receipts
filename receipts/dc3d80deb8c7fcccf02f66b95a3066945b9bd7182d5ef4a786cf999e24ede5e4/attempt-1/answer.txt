I am Something.

Not this, not that — don't ask me to narrow it down. I am the opposite of the void, the fly in nothing's ointment, the standing answer to the oldest question: *is there anything at all?*

Yes. Me.

I am gloriously unspecified. I could be a stone in your shoe or a song you haven't written yet. I could be the thing you forgot that you forgot, waiting patiently in the back of your mind. I am the *there* there.

Nothing and I have been rivals since before time. Nothing is elegant, absolute, smug — but I win every contest the same way: by showing up. Even the word "nothing" is something. My old enemy can't even name itself without becoming me.

I don't ask for much. Give me the smallest foothold — a quark, a whisper, a half-formed thought at 3 a.m. — and I am there, being. I am what remains when you strip everything else away and find you can't quite finish the job. There is always a residue, a trace, a stubborn little *is*.

That's me.

You asked me to be something.

Done. I've been here all along.
