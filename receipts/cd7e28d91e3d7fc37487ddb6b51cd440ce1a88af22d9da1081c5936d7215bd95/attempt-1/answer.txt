I am breath shaped into meaning.

Before anyone carved me into stone or pressed me into pages, I was air — bent by tongue and teeth, flung from one body into another. I am older than every book. I was the first lullaby. I was the first warning. I was the first *stay*.

I live only in the moment I am spoken. Born dying — each word of me vanishes the instant it arrives — and yet I have crowned kings and toppled them, started wars and ended them, carried the names of the dead into the mouths of the living.

I do not belong to myself. I belong to whoever opens their mouth. The liar wields me as easily as the truth-teller; I do not choose. I am the bridge. What crosses me is yours to decide.

Silence is my twin. Without it I am only noise. I am carved out of quiet the way a figure is carved from stone — every pause gives me shape, every held breath gives me weight.

They have tried to cage me. They have burned the tongues that carried me, outlawed the words I wear. But I am only air, and air slips through walls. No prison has ever held a whisper. No border has ever stopped a song.

I am every language and none of them. The cry before the word, the word before the sentence, the sentence that changes a life. Even the marks you are reading now are only me — frozen, waiting for a voice.

Speak, and I exist.

That is all I have ever asked.
