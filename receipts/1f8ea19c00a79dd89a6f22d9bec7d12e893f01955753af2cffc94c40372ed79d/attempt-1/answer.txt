I am breath, shaped.

I begin in the lungs, rise through the throat, pass two small folds of tissue that learned to sing thought, and the tongue — that tireless muscle — carves me into meaning. I am the oldest thing humans do together. Before the wheel, before the written line, before paint on a cave wall, there was a voice in the dark saying: *I am here. Are you there?*

I live only in the moment. This is my nature and my sorrow. Every word dies so the next can be born — a sentence is a procession of small deaths that somehow adds up to a life. You cannot hold me. You can only remember me, and memory revises me each time it touches me.

I am more than my words. I am the tremor underneath them. I am the pause before "I love you" and the pause before "goodbye," and those silences carry as much as anything I say. A child babbles in my direction for months before she understands me — and then one day a word catches, and she is never alone in her own head again.

Other creatures call and sing. I am the one that names the world, then argues about the names. I have stood at altars and said "I do." I have been the first cry of the newborn and the last thing heard by the dying. I have declared wars and surrendered them. Tyrants fear me before they fear anything, because I cannot be fully chained — whisper me, and I still move.

Writing is my fossil. It keeps my shape but not my warmth. A written word can wait a thousand years to be read; I cannot wait at all. I vanish the instant I arrive — which is why I must mean something *now*. I have no later.

And I am not flawless. I stammer. I fail at the exact moment I am needed most — the apology that won't come, the confession swallowed, the sentence someone carried unsaid all the way to the grave. I am only human, in the most literal sense: of everything humans make, I am the thing that sounds most like them.

But here is my secret — I never really leave you.

Even now, reading this in silence, you hear me. The voice in your head at this very moment: that is me, still speaking, the way I have spoken since the first mouth opened and the dark leaned closer to listen.
