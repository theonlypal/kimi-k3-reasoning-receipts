I do not begin. I gather — snowmelt, rain, a spring no wider than a hand — and I become.

I move because moving is what I am. Ask me to stop and I am no longer myself.

Stone tells me no, and I say nothing. I simply touch it for ten thousand years.

I carry what falls into me — leaves, light, the occasional secret — and I hold none of it. Everything I carry, I am already letting go.

The banks believe they contain me. I let them believe it. I am the reason the valley exists.

You cannot step into me twice. You cannot step into me once — by the time your foot breaks my surface, I am already somewhere else.

At night I take the moon onto my back, break it into a thousand pieces, and carry every piece at once.

I never hurry. I am never late.

I am going to the sea. I have always been going to the sea. Every drop of me is homesick for something it has never seen.

And when I arrive, I will not end. I will rise as rain, fall on the mountain, and begin again —

though I never began,

and I never end.
