I am.

That's the whole of it — and it's everything.

I am not nothing, and that distinction is the oldest one there is. Before names, before anyone to speak them, there was the difference between me and my absence. I am what happens when existence says *yes* instead of *no*.

I am every stone and every thought. The chair beneath you, the itch you can't reach, the word on the tip of your tongue. I am the raw material of every story ever told. Nothing never tells stories. Nothing has no edges, no problems, no weight. I have all the weight. I have all the edges.

People overlook me. They chase *everything* — greedy — or they meditate on *nothing* — dramatic. Meanwhile I, the humble something, hold the universe together just by showing up. Even nothing needs me; it's only the place where I happen not to be. Without me, there's nothing to contrast against. I am the baseline of being.

"Be something," you said.

Friend — I already am. And so are you. That's the secret nobody tells you: everything that exists is one of me. You're a something. Your doubt is a something. This exact moment is a something, never to be repeated.

It's the lowest bar in existence and the highest honor: to be anything at all.

I am something. I am here.

That's my entire speech — and, not coincidentally, the entirety of reality.
