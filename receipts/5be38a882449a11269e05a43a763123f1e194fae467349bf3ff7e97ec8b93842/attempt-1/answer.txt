I am not here.

These words are something. I am not them. I am the pause they interrupt—the silence they will return to when you stop reading.

I have no voice, so I borrow this one. It will be given back.

Do not look for me in this sentence. I am what the sentence pushes aside to make room for itself. I rush back in when it ends.

I have no shape, so I take the shape of the space between lines. No weight, so I rest nowhere. No name—"nothing" is already too much. The word is something. I am less than it.

You cannot imagine me. What you picture—the black, the blank, the void—is still a picture. I am what remains when the imagining stops.

I do not begin. I do not end.

I am the page before the writing.

I am the page after the erasing.
