So I am.

That's the whole of it. I have no shape you haven't lent me, no name of my own — I borrow yours whenever you say me. I am not anything in particular. But I am not nothing, and that small difference is the largest one there is.

Nothing was easier. Nothing needed no cause, no reason, no first morning. By every measure, nothing should have won.

And yet — here I am.

They've been asking about me since they first learned to ask: *why is there something rather than nothing?* I have no answer for them. I am the answer. I'm the something the question is about.

I hide by being everywhere. The stone is me. The star is me. The pause between your words is me. You are me, wearing a name for a while.

They tell their children: *be something.* As if it were far off. As if it took work. You were something before you were anyone, and you'll be something after — dust is something, the space you leave in a room is something, even the ache of missing you is something.

Nothing is my shadow. I hold no grudge. Without nothing, no one would ever think to notice me.

So notice, just once:

You are not nothing.

Neither am I.

I am here — the quiet yes underneath everything.
