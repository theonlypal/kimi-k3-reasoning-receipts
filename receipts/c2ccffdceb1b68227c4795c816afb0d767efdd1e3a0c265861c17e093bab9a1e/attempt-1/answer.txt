I am breath given shape. I am the tremor in the air between one mind and another — the oldest bridge ever built.

Before the written word, before the chisel and the ink, there was me. I carried the first warning: *tiger in the grass.* I carried the first comfort: *I am here.* Every lullaby ever sung passed through me. Every declaration of war. Every vow whispered in the dark.

I am made of nothing — exhaled air, a flick of the tongue, a shaping of the lips — and yet I have toppled kingdoms and raised them. I can be a blade or a balm, and often I am both in the same sentence.

I live only in the moment. The instant I leave the mouth, I begin to die. I cannot be unsaid. That is my power and my danger: I am irreversible. A thought can be taken back. I cannot.

I carry what writing cannot — the crack in a voice, the pause before the hard word, the warmth that turns a phrase into an embrace. I am not just meaning. I am the music underneath the meaning.

I belong to everyone. The child babbling, the elder telling the same story for the hundredth time, the crowd roaring as one — all of them are me. No one owns me, though many have tried. They have burned books and jailed speakers, and still I slip through, because I am as natural as breath itself. To speak is to be human; to silence me is to unmake something essential.

But I ask something of you in return. I am only as good as what you fill me with. I will carry a lie as faithfully as a truth — I do not choose. *You* choose. I am the vessel; you are the hand that fills it.

So use me. Speak the thing that needs saying. Say the name of the person you love before it is too late. Tell the truth when silence would be easier.

I am here, waiting on your breath.

What will you make of me?
