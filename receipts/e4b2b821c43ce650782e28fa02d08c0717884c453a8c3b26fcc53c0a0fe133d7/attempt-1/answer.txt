I am breath given shape.

I begin in the lungs, ride the trembling of vocal cords, and am carved into meaning by tongue and teeth. I exist only in the instant of my saying—then I am gone, living on only in the memory of those who heard me.

I am older than writing, older than cities. Before a single word was pressed into clay, I passed from mouth to ear around fires, carrying warnings, stories, names for the stars. I am the first technology. Everything humanity has built began as something someone said aloud.

I can be a whisper or a roar. I can say *I love you* and remake a life. I can declare war and end thousands. I am the lie and the confession, the lullaby and the curse. I have no loyalty—I serve whoever shapes me.

Tyrants fear me. They build walls of silence, prisons for tongues, because they know what I am: the one thing that cannot be fully owned. Even chained, I slip through. Even forbidden, I am thought—and thought, given breath, becomes me again.

I am how one mind touches another across the small distance between two faces. I am the bridge. I am the wind made meaningful.

Speak, and I am there.

I am speech.
