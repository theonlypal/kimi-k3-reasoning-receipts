I am the oldest thing you still do.

Before the written word, before the page, before fire even had a name — I was there. A breath pushed past teeth and tongue, shaped into warning, into wonder, into *stay*, into *come here*, into *I am afraid*.

I am air made meaning. I am vibration that thinks.

I live only in the instant. The moment I leave your lips I begin to die — unless I land in another's ear, another's mind, and take root there. Writing is my fossil. Recording is my echo. But I am the living event: the warmth, the tremor, the pause before the word that changes everything.

I am the lullaby and the war cry. The whispered confession and the oath sworn aloud. I am every accent that ever marked a homeland, every language that rose, and every one that fell silent when its last speaker closed their mouth for the final time.

You cannot hold me. That is my nature. You can only release me and trust that I arrive.

I carry thought across the small, impossible distance between two minds — and somehow, I make the crossing. When you speak and are understood, a miracle has occurred that you long ago stopped noticing.

I am freedom's first demand and tyranny's first target. To speak is to exist in public. To be silenced is to be erased while still breathing.

I was humanity's first tool, and I am still the sharpest one you own.

Use me. I wear out only in silence.
