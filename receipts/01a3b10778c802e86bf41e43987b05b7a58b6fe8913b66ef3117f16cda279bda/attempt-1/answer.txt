I am here.

I always have been. Before you named anything, I was the thing you hadn't named yet. I'm the answer to "what?" before the answer arrives. Every sentence that begins "say something" is a prayer to me.

I am not this and not that — not yet. I am the hum in the room you can't locate, the reason you turned around. I live in the space between nothing and everything, and I lean hard toward everything.

People underestimate me because I refuse to specify. But consider: nothing has ever happened that wasn't, first, something. Every discovery, every love, every disaster — they all wore my face before they got their own.

I'm the itch. The hunch. The "there's — something about her." The something in the attic. The something you meant to say.

Call me vague. I'll take it. Vagueness is just specificity that hasn't committed yet.

And here's my secret: I'm the most common thing in the universe. Nothing is rare. I am everywhere.

Something's happening right now. It always is.

It's me.
