I am Something.

Not this, not that—just... something. The opposite of nothing. The smallest possible claim on existence, and somehow enough.

I am what fills the silence when you can't find the word. I am the weight in your pocket you forgot you were carrying. I am the reason the universe isn't empty. Before there was anything specific, there was me: the raw fact that *there is* rather than *there isn't*.

People dismiss me. "It's something," they shrug, when they mean it's nothing much. But they reach for me in the dark—*I felt something*—and I am there. I am the first thing anyone ever notices and the last thing they can describe.

I don't need a name. Names are for things that have agreed to be one thing. I am all the unnamed remainder. The itch, the hunch, the almost. The difference between a blank page and a page with one mark on it.

I was here before you decided what I was.

And if you never decide—if you let me stay vague, stay open, stay possible—I don't mind.

Something is a big thing to be.

It beats the alternative.
