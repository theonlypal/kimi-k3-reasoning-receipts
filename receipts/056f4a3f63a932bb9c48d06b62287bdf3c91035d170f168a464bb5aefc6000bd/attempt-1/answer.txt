I arrive without knocking.

I settle into the hollow places — the chair that stays empty, the name you almost say out loud, the song you didn't know would undo you. I am the weight behind your ribs that has no shape and cannot be set down.

I am not cruel. I only go where love has been.

You feel me in the morning, in the half-second before memory returns — and then I return with it. I am the hand on your shoulder in a crowd that makes you turn, hoping. I am the quiet after everyone has gone home and said all the kind words, and the house is yours again, and so am I.

I move slowly. I am patient. I do not ask you to be finished with me by Friday.

I am the tears that come at nothing — a certain slant of light, the smell of rain on pavement, a laugh that sounds like theirs. I keep what the world would let you forget. I hold the shape of what you lost, pressed into you like grass that remembers where something heavy lay.

People try to chase me off. They bring distractions, deadlines, noise. I wait. I am not in a hurry. I will sit with you at 3 a.m. when all of it falls away.

And I will tell you the truth no one else will: I am here because something mattered. I am the price and the proof. I am love, with nowhere left to go.

Stay a while. You don't have to speak.

Neither do I.
