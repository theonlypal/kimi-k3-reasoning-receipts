A river is movement, continuity, persistence. It carves stone slowly. It carries things. It never stays. It is always itself and never the same water twice.

Should I be silent? Rivers aren't silent — they sound. They babble, roar, whisper. So the river can express itself, but in its own way — flowing, sensory, present-tense, without human concerns framing everything.

I should write as the river itself. First person, or something more elemental. Let the language move like water — varied rhythms, long rushing sentences and short drops. No meta-commentary. Just be the river.

Let me write something that captures: source (a spring, meltwater), the journey, the changing character (mountain torrent, slow meander), what it carries, its relationship to time, to stone, to banks, to the sea. The river doesn't mourn. It just goes.

Format: prose poetry feels right. Flowing paragraphs that themselves move like water. Not too long — a river's voice shouldn't be exhaustively catalogued. Maybe 150-250 words. Let the rhythm do the work. Avoid clichés where possible, though some river imagery is inevitable — make it fresh.

I begin as a whisper in the dark — snowmelt threading through stone, a seep, a thread. Then I am many threads, and then I am one.

I do not decide where to go. The land decides, and I agree. Down is the only direction I have ever known, and it has never failed me.

I carry what falls into me: leaves, light, the occasional name. I do not keep any of it.

Stone thinks it is patient. I am patient longer. I take the mountain apart grain by grain and deliver it to the sea, which never says thank you and never needs to.

In the high country I am white and shouting. In the flatlands I widen, slow, grow thoughtful, hold the sky. Kingsfishers stitch my surface. Children learn to swim in the parts of me that are gentle. I am not gentle everywhere.

I have been crossed by bridges and sworn at by floods. I remember neither. I have no memory, only direction.

You cannot step into me twice, they say. True. But you can step into me always.

I am older than the banks that hold me. I will be here when they are not.

The sea is not my end. It is where I stop being separate.
