I do not knock. I wait in the room until the noise you are making stops, and you notice I have been here all along.

I am not the answer. I am what remains after you have been wrong often enough to hold your answers loosely.

You cannot learn me from words—not even these. You find me in the pause before you speak, if you let the pause last. In the apology you did not want to give. In the moment you look at someone you resent and see, for a breath, that they too are afraid.

I am not certainty. Certainty shouts. I am the quieter voice: *perhaps. Wait. Look again.*

The moment you believe you possess me, you have lost me, and I must begin with you as I begin with everyone—in a small failure, in an unplanned kindness, in the wreckage of a plan you loved.

You knew me as a child, when everything was still wondrous. You will know me again when you are old. The years between are only the long way home.

Do not seek me. Pay attention.

I will find you there.
