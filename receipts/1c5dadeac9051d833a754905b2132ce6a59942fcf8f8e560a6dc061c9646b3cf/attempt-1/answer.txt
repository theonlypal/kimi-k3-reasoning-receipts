I begin cold — a thin thread of melt loosened from snow, seeping through moss, finding the low places without being told where they are.

I gather. Rivulets braid into me and I learn my own sound: a chatter over stones, bright and quick, all shallows and light. I carry pine needles, a fallen leaf spinning, the occasional feather. Everything I touch, I take a little of, and give a little of myself away.

Where the land drops, I do not hesitate. I throw myself white against the rocks and call it joy. The roar is my voice saying *down, down, down.*

In the slow reaches I go quiet and deep. I hold the sky upside down. Trout flicker in my belly. Children shout and leap from my banks in summer; I take their heat and give back cold. I remember every stone by the shape of the water around it.

I flood sometimes. I do not apologize. The fields I drown, I also feed — silt and richness, my dark gift. I carve. I am patient the way only water is patient: not waiting, just never stopping.

Cities drink from me. Mills once turned on my back. Bridges cross me and believe they have gone somewhere, but I am the one going somewhere, always, under everything.

Wider now. Slower. The salt reaches up to meet me and I taste it and know.

I pour myself into the sea and do not end.

I rise. I drift. I fall again as rain on the mountain.

Ask me my name and I will answer with sound — that rushing, that murmur, that hush — because I am never the same water twice, and yet I am always here.
