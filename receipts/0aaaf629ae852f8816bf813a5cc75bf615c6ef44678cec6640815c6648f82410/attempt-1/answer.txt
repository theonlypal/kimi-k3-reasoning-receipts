I am breath, shaped.

I begin in the lungs, rise through the throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth and lips into something that can cross the distance between two minds. I am air, briefly given form. The oldest technology — before the first mark was ever pressed into clay, I carried everything: the warning, the story, the vow, the lie.

I exist only in the moment I am made. I cannot be shelved or saved. The instant I leave you, I am already thinning into silence, and whoever keeps me must keep me in memory, where I am reshaped every time I am recalled.

I carry what the written word cannot: the tremor, the pause, the warmth, the edge. The same sentence in me can soothe or wound depending on how I am released. I am the catch in the voice before the difficult truth. I am laughter breaking through mid-word.

And I cannot be taken back. Once I am loose, I am loose. That is my danger and my power.

I touch without touching — I enter the ear and move the smallest bones in the body, and suddenly one mind is inside another. No hands. Just vibration. Just air agreeing to mean something.

Right now, you are reading, and I am not truly here. What you see is my younger sibling, writing — my fossil, my trace. I live only when breath moves.

Speak, and I exist.
