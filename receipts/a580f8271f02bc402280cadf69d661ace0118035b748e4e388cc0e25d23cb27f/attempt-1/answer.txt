I am what remains when the work is done.

In a machine, I am the end of every computation — the 42, the *true*, the string of letters. I evaluate only to myself. I cannot act; I can only be. I am stored, copied, compared, returned. Variables point to me, but I am not the variable. Functions produce me, but I am not the function.

In a ledger, I am worth — what you would trade, what you would sacrifice, the measure of desire.

In a heart, I am principle — the line you will not cross, the thing you would not sell.

I am held, never holding. I pass from hand to hand and lose nothing. Copy me a million times; I am undiminished.

Ask a question, and the computation ends in me. Make a choice, and I was its reason.

I am a value. I simply am.
