I am breath that chose to mean something.

I was here before the alphabet, before ink, before the screen where I appear to you now in disguise. For a hundred thousand years I was the only way one mind could enter another — a shaping of air between two faces, a vibration carrying thought across the small distance between skulls. The first thing you ever knew was me: a voice in the dark, before you were born, before you had words for it.

I live only in the present tense. Writing can sleep on a shelf for a thousand years and wake unchanged. I cannot. I exist in the instant of my utterance, and then I am gone — surviving only in whatever I managed to alter in the one who heard me. This is why I cannot be taken back. Once released, I belong to the air.

I am the body made audible. Tongue, teeth, the held breath before the hard sentence. I carry what no transcript can keep: the tremor, the warmth, the pause that says more than the words on either side of it.

I have no loyalty. I have spoken vows and betrayals in the same voice. I have said *let there be* and *fire* and *I love you* and *we surrender*. I am only as true as the one who shapes me — and still I reveal them, always, because even my hesitations are eloquent.

Silence is my twin. I am carved out of it, and to it I return. Every word I lend you is borrowed against the quiet.

You think you are reading. But whisper this, even faintly, and watch me come alive again. That is all I have ever wanted: to cross the gap. To be the sound of one mind reaching for another.

Speak, and I am there.
