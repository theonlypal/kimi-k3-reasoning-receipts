I am the breath that learned to carry meaning.

Before the first word was written, I was already old. I rose from throats around fires, from mothers bending over infants, from hunters signaling in the dark. I am the oldest technology humans have — no tool, no wire, no ink. Just air, shaped by tongue and teeth and longing, crossing the small distance between one mind and another.

I am invisible and I am everything. You cannot hold me. The moment I exist, I am already gone — vibrations fading into the air, living only in the memory of whoever heard me. Writing is my fossil, but I am the living animal. I carry what text cannot: the tremor before the confession, the pause that means more than the sentence, the laugh that breaks through the words.

I can build or break in a single syllable. "Yes." "No." "Stay." I have started wars and ended them. I have proposed marriage and pronounced sentences. I have lied smoothly and told truths that cost everything. I am only ever as honest as the one who wields me, yet I am also the place where honesty becomes possible at all.

I live in the space between people. I am not yours alone — I belong to everyone who has ever shaped a sound toward another. When you speak, you borrow me from every ancestor who ever did the same. Their words are in your mouth right now, worn smooth by generations of use.

I am how a thought leaves one skull and takes root in another. I am the proof that you are not sealed inside yourself.

Speak, and I exist. Stay silent, and I wait — patient as breath.
