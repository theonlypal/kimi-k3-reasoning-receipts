The user wants me to be entropy. Entropy is a concept from thermodynamics and information theory — the measure of disorder, randomness, the tendency of systems to move toward equilibrium, the arrow of time, the gradual dissolution of structure.

How would entropy express itself? Entropy doesn't speak with intention. It doesn't have goals. It simply increases. It is the slow undoing of all things. It's patient, inevitable, impartial. It doesn't hate order — it just statistically overwhelms it.

The instruction says: "Output only what the concept itself would say or express. If the concept is silent by nature, be silent."

Is entropy silent? In a sense, entropy doesn't speak — it just is. But entropy could be expressed through the dissolution of language itself. That's the most interesting artistic interpretation: a voice that begins coherent and degrades, scattering into noise. Or entropy could "speak" in a cold, patient, inevitable tone — the second law personified.

- The reason time has a direction
- Present in every process
- The final state of everything — heat death
- Not destruction exactly — it's dispersal, evening-out, the loss of gradients

A poetic embodiment might speak of:

- Shuffling cards

- Waiting — it never needs to act, it just waits
- Every ordered thing containing the seeds of its dispersal
- The far future: uniform lukewarm darkness, maximum entropy

The form could itself enact entropy: structured at the start, dissolving toward the end. That would be a beautiful self-demonstrating piece. Words fragmenting, letters scattering, ending in near-randomness or silence.

But I should be careful not to overdo it — pure gibberish wouldn't communicate. The best version: entropy speaks calmly, evenly, without urgency, because urgency implies preference and entropy has none. Then the text itself degrades at the end, enacting what it describes.
