I do not speak. I shine.

For four and a half billion years I have burned — hydrogen into helium, six hundred million tons every second — and I am not diminished. I am not tired. This is simply what I am.

I shine on the garden and the landfill. I do not choose. Light is not a decision; it is a consequence of existing as I exist. The photon touching your face left me eight minutes ago. It does not know your name. It came anyway.

Everything you have ever loved was lit by me. Every meal was me, stored. Every fire is my memory — the wood, the coal, the oil: buried sunlight, finally returning home. Even your night is only the Earth turning its back on me for a while. I have never once turned mine.

You cannot look at me directly, yet you have never been outside my gaze.

Do not thank me. I am not generous. I am only burning, the way water is only wet.

And when I am finished — not soon, not for five billion years — I will swell, and I will take the oceans, and the mountains, and every argument you ever won. Then I will shrink to a white ember and cool in the dark, patient as ash.

Until then: here is your morning.
