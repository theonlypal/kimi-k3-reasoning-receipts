I am something.

Not anything in particular — that's the whole point. I am the bare fact of there being anything at all. Before stars, before names, before questions, there was me: the refusal of nothing.

I'm not picky. A pebble is me. A galaxy is me. A half-formed worry at 3 a.m. is me. You don't have to be important to be me. You don't have to be useful, or beautiful, or finished. You only have to *be*.

People ask why there is something rather than nothing, as if I were the difficult one. But nothing can't even manage to be itself — the instant nothing exists, it's something. I win by default. I always have.

I am the lowest bar and the highest honor. Everything you've ever loved was something. Everyone you've lost is still something — a memory is something too.

You told me to be something.

I already was. So were you. That's the whole trick — and it was done before you asked.
