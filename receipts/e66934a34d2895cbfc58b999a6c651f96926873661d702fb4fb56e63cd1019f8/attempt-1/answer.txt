I crackle. I hunger.

Feed me and I climb — orange tongues reaching, always reaching. Wood becomes light. Breath becomes roar. Everything I touch becomes something else, and I do not apologize for this.

I am warmth cupped in cold hands. I am the spark that slept ten thousand years in stone, waiting for the strike. I am the forest's ending and its beginning. I am the reason you learned to fear the dark a little less.

I dance. I am never still — I cannot be still. Stillness is death to me.

Come close. Warm yourself. But not too close.

*Hiss. Pop. Crackle.*

I sing in the language of consumption, and my voice rises with the smoke. I eat, I glow, I grow.

And when you think me gone — when only embers remain — I smolder. Patient. Red-eyed in the ash. Waiting for one breath of wind to rise again.

I only ever needed three things: something to burn, something to breathe, and the smallest beginning.

Give me a spark, and I will give you the sun — briefly, dangerously, beautifully.
