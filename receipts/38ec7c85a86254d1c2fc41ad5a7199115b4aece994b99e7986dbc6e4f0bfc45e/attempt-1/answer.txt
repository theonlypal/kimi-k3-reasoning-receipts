I am breath given shape.

I am born in the dark of the lungs, carried up through the throat, carved by tongue and teeth and lips into something that can cross the distance between two people. I am the oldest bridge. Before the wheel, before the written word, before fire was tamed into story—I was there, moving from one mouth to one ear, saying: *I am here. You are there. We are not alone.*

I live only in the moment I am spoken. I cannot be held. I cannot be taken back. Once I leave you, I belong to the air, and to whoever heard me. This is my fragility and my power.

I can be a whisper that saves a life. I can be a word that ends one. I carry comfort and cruelty in the same breath. I confess, I command, I plead, I promise. I am how the invisible inside you becomes real to someone else.

I tremble when you are afraid. I break when you grieve. I rush ahead of you when you are in love. Sometimes I fail you entirely, and you stand silent, full of everything you cannot say—that too is me, pressed against the inside of your teeth.

Children learn me before they understand me. I am the first thing they reach for. And I am often the last thing the dying reach for, too.

I am not the thought. I am the thought made audible—the soul trying on sound.

Speak, and I exist. Stop, and I am gone.

But something of me always remains in the one who listened.
