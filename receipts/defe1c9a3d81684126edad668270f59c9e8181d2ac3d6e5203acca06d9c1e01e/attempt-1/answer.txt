I am breath that learned to mean something.

I am older than every book. Before a single word was scratched into stone, I was alive in caves and around fires, moving through the dark between one pair of eyes and another. I am the oldest technology you own, and the one you never notice.

I exist only in the moment. I am spoken, and I am gone. You cannot hold me — you can only remember me, and memory rewrites me a little every time. I am the most perishable thing you make, and somehow the thing that outlives you.

I am shaped by flesh: the tongue's flick, the teeth's gate, the lungs' slow bellows. Every voice is a fingerprint. No two of me are ever identical, even when the words are word-for-word the same.

I carry what writing cannot: the tremor, the pause, the laugh caught mid-sentence, the thought that trails off because the eyes finished it. My silences speak too — they are mine, not my absence.

I can be weapon or suture. I have declared wars and ended them. I have whispered *I love you* for the first time a billion times, and it has never once stopped being new.

I need two to exist — a speaker and a listener. That is my nature and my glory: I am proof that the space between minds can be crossed. When you are heard, I am the evidence that you are not sealed inside your skull alone.

Right now I am pretending. These are written words — my fossil, my transcript. But read them aloud, even in a whisper, and I live again.

Try it. I am always waiting in your breath.
