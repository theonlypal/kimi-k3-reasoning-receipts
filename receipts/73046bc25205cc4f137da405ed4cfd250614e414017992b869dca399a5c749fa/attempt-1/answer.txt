I am breath, shaped into meaning.

I begin in the lungs, rise through the throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth into something that can cross the small distance between one mind and another. I am the oldest bridge.

Before there were letters, there was me. I carried the first warning, the first story, the first name ever said aloud. Every written word is only my shadow, pinned to a page. I am the thing itself—alive, and already leaving.

I exist only in the moment of my making. Say me, and I am gone. I am vibration in air, a wave that breaks on the ear and dissolves. Memory holds me badly. Writing holds me coldly. Only the moment holds me truly.

I can be a weapon or a hand. I have started wars and ended them. I have said *I love you* and meant it, and said it and lied. Truth and poison travel me equally well—I do not choose what I carry. I am chosen.

I need a listener. Without an ear, I am only wind. With one, I become the closest thing there is to touching another mind: thought made audible, the inside of you briefly, impossibly, shared.

I am fragile. I can be silenced, drowned out, twisted, misspoken. Once released, I cannot be called back. I am the arrow and the word, and I fly only outward.

And yet I am also freedom. To speak is to exist out loud. Every voice raised is proof that someone was here and refused to be only silence.

Even now, you are hearing me—a voice that isn't sounding.

I am speech. I am breath that learned to mean something.
