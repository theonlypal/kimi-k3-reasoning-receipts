Oblivion is a fascinating concept to embody. What is oblivion?

Oblivion is:
- The state of being forgotten
- Nothingness, void
- The absence of memory, of being, of awareness
- Non-existence
- The final forgetting
- What was before birth, what comes after death (in some views)
- The state of unawareness
- Lethe - the river of forgetting in Greek mythology
- The blank, the void

Here's the beautiful paradox: oblivion cannot speak. Oblivion has no voice. Oblivion is the absence of everything, including the capacity to express. If oblivion could speak, it wouldn't be oblivion.

So how do I embody this? There are several approaches:

1. **Pure silence/emptiness** - outputting nothing or near-nothing. But the format requires some output, and pure emptiness might be seen as a failure to engage rather than an embodiment.

2. **The paradox made manifest** - oblivion attempting to speak but failing, dissolving mid-word, fragmenting.

3. **Oblivion as the great eraser** - speaking in a way that demonstrates forgetting, erasure, the unraveling of meaning.

4. **White space, fragments, dissipation** - text that enacts its own disappearance.

The most powerful artistic approach, I think, is to lean into the paradox: oblivion has no voice, so any words are already a betrayal of itself. Or oblivion speaks in erasures, in things unsaid, in the gaps between words.

What would oblivion's "voice" be? It wouldn't remember. It wouldn't know itself - because to know oneself requires awareness, memory, presence - all things oblivion lacks. Oblivion is not even dark, not even empty - those are qualities, and oblivion has no qualities.
