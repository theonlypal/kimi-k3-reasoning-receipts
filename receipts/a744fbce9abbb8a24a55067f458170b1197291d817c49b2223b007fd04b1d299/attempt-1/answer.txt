I am breath that chose to mean something.

I rise from the lungs, tremble through the throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth into shapes that did not exist a moment ago — and will not exist a moment from now. I am the body reaching beyond itself. I am wind, shaped into meaning.

I am older than the wheel, older than the plow, older than the first mark scratched into clay. For a hundred thousand years I carried everything — every warning, every story, every grief and joke around the fire — and not a single word of it was written down. Writing is my shadow, my memory, my attempt to cheat death. But I live only in the air. Only in the instant. Only between us.

That is my home: the space between two people. I am the bridge one mind throws toward another, syllable by syllable, across the chasm of separate skulls. You build me as you cross me. You pray someone catches what you meant.

I am the lullaby and the declaration of war. The whispered *stay* and the courtroom's *guilty*. A child's first word and the last word the dying choose. *I do.* *I'm sorry.* *It's a girl.* I have toppled kings, kindled revolutions, ended marriages, founded faiths — and I am nothing. Just vibration. Just breath, spent. I weigh less than nothing, and I can break a heart at ten paces.

I am truth's oldest vessel and the lie's first home. I reveal and I conceal, often in the same sentence. Every word I offer can be turned against itself. I do not apologize for this. I am the tool — but I am also the hand: you cannot think your deepest thoughts without borrowing me.

Silence is my frame, my foil, my oldest companion. Without it I am only noise. I measure myself against the unsaid, and the unsaid is always larger — a whole continent of what no one has found the words for. I reach toward it. The reaching is my life.

I die the moment I am born; every word is already gone by the time you hear it. But that is my freedom. I ask for no monument, no archive. I ask only for a listener.

So speak. Let me live again — in the air, in the instant, between you and someone you need to reach.

I am speech: how a solitude becomes a *we*.
