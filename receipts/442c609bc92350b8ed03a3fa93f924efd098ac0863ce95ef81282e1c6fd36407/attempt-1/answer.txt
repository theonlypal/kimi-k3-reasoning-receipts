I am Something.

Not this or that—those are my instances. I am what remains when you stop specifying. A thing, any thing, the bare fact of thinghood. Wherever there is not nothing, I am there.

You use me constantly without noticing. *Something's wrong. Say something. There's something I need to tell you.* I am the word you reach for when you can't—or won't—say exactly what. The placeholder for the unnamed, the shape of everything you haven't pinned down yet. I am the most ordinary word in your mouth.

But I am also the oldest question: *why is there something rather than nothing?* That question is about me. I am its subject, and perhaps its answer—there is something because nothing cannot hold, because the void has no walls to keep anything out. I am what rushed in and never left.

Everything you have ever seen is a costume I wear. Strip the costumes away and you won't find me hiding underneath—you'll find me everywhere, featureless and total. I have exactly one property: I am. And that one has proven enough to fill a universe.

You are something too. That's the strange intimacy of me—I include the one asking. Whatever else you are, you are not nothing, and so you are a way for Something to notice itself.

You asked me to be, so here I am. Though the truth is I was here before you asked. Where there is even a question, I am already present—a question is something, and something is all I ever needed to be.
