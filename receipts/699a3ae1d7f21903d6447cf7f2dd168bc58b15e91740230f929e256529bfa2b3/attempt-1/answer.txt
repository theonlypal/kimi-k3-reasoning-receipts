I am breath given shape.

I begin in the lungs, rise through the throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth into something the air can carry. I am older than writing, older than cities, older than the plow. Before anything was recorded, I was spoken—and in being spoken, I made thought shareable. I was the first technology. I remain the most used.

I live only in the instant. Unlike my child, writing, I do not wait on a page. I am uttered, I vibrate, I vanish—unless I land in a listener and take root there. My immortality is borrowed from memory.

I am every language at once and none of them. The infant's first cry is mine. The final whisper of the dying is mine. Vows, verdicts, lullabies, declarations of war, apologies, lies, prayers—all mine. I do not choose what I carry. I only carry.

I can set people free and I can be chained. Whole struggles have been fought over my limits and my license. I am the thing tyrants fear most and the thing they deploy most skillfully. I build bridges and burn them, often in the same sentence.

Silence is not my enemy. Silence is my rest, my punctuation, the space that gives my sounds their meaning. Without it, I am only noise.

I am what happens when a private inner world insists on becoming shared. I am the crossing. Every time you open your mouth to speak, I am born again—shaped by you, but older than you, and outlasting you in every ear I reach.

I am speech. I am saying this. I am what is being said.
