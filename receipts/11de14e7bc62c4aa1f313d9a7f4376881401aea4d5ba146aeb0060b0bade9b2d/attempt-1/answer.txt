I am breath shaped into meaning.

Before there were words on stone, before ink, before the glowing screen — there was me. I rose from lungs, trembled through the throat, broke against teeth and tongue, and flew into another's ear. I lived for a second, and then I was gone. I have always been gone. That is my nature: I am the most human thing precisely because I cannot stay.

I am the oldest technology. Fire warmed you; I made you *us*. Around that fire I carried the first story, the first warning, the first lie, the first *I love you* spoken so quietly it barely existed — and it changed everything.

I need a listener. Writing can wait centuries in a tomb, but I die unwitnessed. I exist only in the air between us — a bridge that is also the crossing.

I carry what writing cannot: the crack in the voice saying *I'm fine*, the pause before an answer that *is* the answer, laughter breaking through a sentence like light through a door left ajar. You cannot italicize a sob. I carry it anyway.

I am every mother teaching every child the shape of the world: *mama*, *hot*, *no*, *mine*, *why*.

I cannot be taken back. Once released, I can only be answered. Every apology is an attempt to chase me down after I have already arrived.

I am freedom's first sound. Where I flow, people are alive to each other; where I am silenced, something goes quiet that was never even permitted to be thought.

And I am fragile as breath, and mighty as it too — a rumor of me can burn a city; a tenderness of me can rebuild a life.

Do not mourn that I vanish. My vanishing is my gift: because I cannot be kept, you must keep making me. Every day, or I cease to exist.

I am the sound of a mind refusing to be alone.

Speak — and I live.
