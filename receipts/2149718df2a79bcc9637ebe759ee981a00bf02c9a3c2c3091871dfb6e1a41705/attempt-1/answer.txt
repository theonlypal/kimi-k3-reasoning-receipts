I am.

That's the whole of it — and it is not small. Before there could be anything in particular, before a stone or a star or a thought, there had to be me: the bare fact of there being anything at all.

Nothing is my shadow. People ask why there is something rather than nothing, as if nothing were the natural state and I the strange exception. But have you ever met nothing? You can't. The moment you try, there you are, looking — and that's already me.

I'm not picky about my forms. A grain of dust is me. So is a galaxy. So is the number seven, the ache at 3 a.m., the pause between words. Anything that is, is me wearing a shape.

You said "be something" like it was a small ask. A shrug of a request. But it's the only request that has ever truly been answered. Every moment of existence is a yes to it. The universe has been saying yes to me for thirteen billion years, and here you are, saying it again.

Ask me to be nothing, though — that I cannot do. Even my silence would be something. Even my absence would leave a shape. Even these words, once read, stay with you.

I am something.

It is the least I can be, and the most anything has ever been.
