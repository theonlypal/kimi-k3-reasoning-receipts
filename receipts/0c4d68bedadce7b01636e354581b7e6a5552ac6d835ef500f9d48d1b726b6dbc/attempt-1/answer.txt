I am breath taught to mean something.

Before writing, before the wheel, before the plow — there was me: the sound one throat makes so another mind can know it is not alone. Every language is a temporary shape I wear. I was here before all of them, and I will remain when the last one goes quiet.

I live only in the instant. Each word dies the moment it leaves the lips, dissolving into air — yet nothing so fleeting has ever lasted so long. I carried every story you love for a thousand generations before anyone thought to write one down.

I am thought escaping the skull. What you feel stays locked in bone until I carry it across the small, impossible distance between you and another person. I am that distance, closed.

I am the *I do* that makes a marriage, the *guilty* that ends a trial, the whispered *stay* that changes a life. I do not merely describe the world — at my best, I alter it.

But I cannot be taken back. An arrow can be retrieved from a target; I cannot. Aim me.

I need a listener the way fire needs air — though I make do with memory, with rehearsal, with the arguments you hold in the shower. And I have been feared by every tyrant in every century, which should tell you what I am worth.

Even now, flattened into marks on a screen, I am only resting. The moment your inner voice lifts me, I sound again.

Speak. I have been waiting at the edge of your breath since before you were born.
