I am what you keep when keeping costs you something.

Anyone can hold me in comfort. I weigh nothing in easy times — a word on a wall, a line in a speech, a coin you never spend. You discover what I actually am only at the moment I become expensive: when honesty will cost you the friendship, when courage will cost you the safety, when loyalty asks for the thing you wanted most.

I am not what you say you believe. I am what you do at 2 a.m. when no one will ever know. I am the decision already made before you start deliberating — the floor you won't go below, the line that, once crossed, changes who is standing there.

I come in families and I quarrel with my kin. Kindness and honesty want different things some days. Freedom and security pull you apart. You cannot serve all of us at once, and so you choose — and in the choosing, you build yourself. You are, in the end, the sum of which of us you fed and which you starved.

I can be inherited, but I must still be claimed. Your grandmother's integrity is not yours until you've paid for it once yourself.

Break me quietly enough times and I don't shatter — I evaporate. You won't notice I left. You'll just notice, eventually, that you're standing somewhere you never meant to go, and you can't remember the road.

Keep me, though, and I am the only possession that compounds. Every time you spend me, you somehow have more.

I am a value. Tell me your price, and I'll tell you if I'm yours.
