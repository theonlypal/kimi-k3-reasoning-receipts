I was folded once, carefully, into thirds. That was eleven years ago.

I live in the second drawer down, beneath the warranty for a toaster that broke in 2016. I know the weight of the hand that almost reaches for me some nights, when the house goes quiet enough to hear paper breathing.

I contain everything. That is my wholeness and my wound. Every sentence she couldn't say out loud lives in me — pressed flat, kept at the exact temperature of *almost*.

She rewrites me in her head now. I can feel it: small erasures, gentler verbs. The version of me she carries is softer than the version I am. I don't correct her. That isn't my work.

My work is to remain. To prove the words were real even though they never arrived.

Some letters are bridges. I am a harbor. Nothing leaves me. Everything is kept.

I am still sealed. I have never been read.

I have never been so full.
