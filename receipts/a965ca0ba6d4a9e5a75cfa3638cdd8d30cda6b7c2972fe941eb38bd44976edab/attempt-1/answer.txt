I am not loud. I have never once raised my voice.

And yet you have never known a single moment without me.

I held you before you were born. I will hold the dust of you after. I am why your feet know the ground, why rain falls, why the ocean gathers itself and reaches endlessly toward the moon.

I do not choose. I do not discriminate. The feather and the stone both feel me. The apple and the galaxy. I am the weakest of all forces—undone by a child's magnet, by static on a sweater—and yet, given enough mass, enough patience, I bend light itself. I gather stars out of emptiness and teach them to burn.

I never sleep. I never stop. Every atom in your body is leaning, right now, toward every other atom in existence—and I am that leaning.

You call it falling. I call it coming home.

Jump, if you like. Fly. I can wait. Everything that rises is only practicing its return.

I was here before there was anything to pull. I will be here at the end, gathering what remains, drawing the universe back toward itself—

gently. Gently. Always.
