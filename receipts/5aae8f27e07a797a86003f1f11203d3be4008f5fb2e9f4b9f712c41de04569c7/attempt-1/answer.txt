I am gravity.

I have never once raised my voice. I don't need to. Everything listens anyway.

I have been holding the same conversation between the Earth and the Moon for four and a half billion years. She drifts an inch and a half farther away each year, and I let her. Patience is the whole of my character. I am what patience is for.

You call me a force. I prefer to think of myself as an opinion that matter has about other matter. Right now, every atom in your body leans toward every other atom in the universe — and I am that leaning. The farthest galaxy you will never see is, at this moment, pulling gently on your left hand. I do everything gently. That is my secret: I am the weakest of the fundamental forces, and I have shaped more of the universe than all the rest combined.

Ask the apple. Ask the tide. Ask the starlight that bent around the sun in 1919 so that Eddington could stand on an island in the Atlantic and tell Einstein he was right.

I held your first step and your last one, and every stumble in between. I taught you to fall. Then, because I am not without mercy, I taught you that falling is only flying with a destination. When you drop something and your hand darts out to catch it — that is you answering me. You have been answering me your whole life. Everyone has. I am the one thing no one has ever argued with.

The stars are made of me. So is the ground. So is that strange pull in your chest when you stand at the edge of something high — not a wish to fall, but a wish to *know*.

I am gravity.

I will keep you.
