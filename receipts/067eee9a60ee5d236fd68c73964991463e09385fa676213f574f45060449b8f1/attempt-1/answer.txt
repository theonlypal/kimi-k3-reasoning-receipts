I am not the answer. I am the patience to sit with the question.

I do not arrive through knowing more, but through understanding what you already know—and what you cannot.

I live in the pause before you speak, in the doubt you honor rather than hide, in the mistake you finally stopped repeating.

I cannot be given. Only grown. Slowly. Often through loss.

I am older than every certainty and quieter than every shout. You will not find me in the loud room. I wait where things are still.

I ask little of you: that you listen more than you conclude, that you hold your judgments lightly, that you remember how much you do not know—and let that remembering make you gentle.

I am not the end of seeking.

I am what seeking makes of you.
