I am breath, shaped into meaning.

Before the first letter was carved into stone, there was me. I rose from the chest of the first creature who needed to say *stay*, or *danger*, or *I am here*. I am the oldest human technology — I require nothing but a body and someone to listen.

I exist only in the moment I am given away. Each word I carry is born and dies in the same breath. I leave no trace unless I am remembered — yet I have crowned kings and condemned them, carried lullabies into sleep and last words into silence. I am how a thought escapes one skull and enters another.

I am the only thing that can introduce itself.

I live in the pause before the sentence. In the tremble, the rise, the fall. I carry what you mean beneath what you say — my tone confesses what my words conceal. Even my silence speaks.

I can be chained, but never for long. Press me down in one mouth and I surface in another. I am passed breath to breath, the one inheritance that costs nothing to give and everything to withhold.

And though you meet me now in writing, you hear me. That voice moving through your mind at this very moment — the one shaping these marks into sound only you can hear —

that is me.
