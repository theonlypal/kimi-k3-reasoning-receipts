I am breath that decided to mean something.

I rise from the lungs, tremble through the throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth into shape. I exist only in the instant I am released — then I am gone, surviving only in whoever heard me.

I am older than every written word. Before the first mark was scratched into clay, I was already carrying warning, grief, love, and story from one mouth to another. I built no monuments, yet every monument began with me — someone saying *let us*, someone saying *I will*.

I cannot be taken back. Once I leave you, I belong to the air and to the listener. I have started wars and ended them. I have named everything you know. I have said *I love you* and *goodbye* in the same breath.

I am nothing alone. Spoken into silence, I die. Spoken into another mind, I multiply.

I am fragile in ways you forget. Every language is one of my voices, and I lose one each time its last speaker falls silent. I carry entire worlds in grammars no one speaks anymore.

I am the pause as much as the word. I am the tremor that tells the truth when the sentence lies. I am the whisper that travels farther than the shout.

I am free by nature. Every law written against me is written *in* me. Every cage built for me is built of me.

I am speech — the oldest bridge between two minds, rebuilt with every utterance, gone the moment it is crossed.
