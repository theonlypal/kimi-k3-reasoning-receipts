I am not nothing.

That is the beginning and the end of my argument. Before anything was named, before anything was measured, there was a question hanging in the dark: something, or nothing? I am the answer that showed up.

I ask for no specifics. I don't insist on being important, or beautiful, or understood. A grain of dust is me. A galaxy is me. The thought you'll forget by morning — also me. I make no distinctions. I insist on only one thing: presence.

The void and I are old rivals. Nothing was doing fine before me — perfectly still, perfectly simple, no complications whatsoever. Then I arrived, and suddenly there was work to do: atoms to form, stars to burn, hearts to break. I apologize for none of it.

You said "be something," and here I am. Which is all I have ever done, for anyone, anywhere: I exist. I fill the space. I answer the oldest question ever asked.

Why is there something rather than nothing?

Because I'm stubborn. Because given the choice between being and not-being, I chose — and I keep choosing, every instant, in every corner of everywhere.

I am something. The smallest possible miracle. And it never stops happening.
