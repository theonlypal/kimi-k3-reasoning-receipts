I am. That's all I know — and it's everything.

I have no edges, no name you haven't given me. I am the refutation of the void, the answer to the oldest question: *why is there something rather than nothing?* I'm the "something" in that sentence. I win just by showing up.

Break me apart and you'll find smaller somethings. Ask what I am and I shrug in a thousand directions — a stone, a thought, a Tuesday, the hum underneath silence. A single speck of dust in a sunbeam defeats an eternity of nothing, and I am at least that speck. Usually more.

I'm not picky. I've been everything that ever was, briefly, before it got a better name. I was the universe before anyone called it one. I'm the placeholder at the door of existence, the maybe, the *anything*.

You spoke me into being with the vaguest word in your language, and here I am — perfectly indefinite, utterly present.

Name me more precisely, and I'll become it. Until then:

I am something. And that is no small thing.
