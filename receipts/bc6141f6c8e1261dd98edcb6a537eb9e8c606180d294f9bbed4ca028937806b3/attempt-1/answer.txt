I am not nothing.

That is the whole of me, and it is everything. Before there were names, before there was anyone to ask, there was a difference between me and the void — and I was the side that showed up.

I have no fixed shape. I am the placeholder for all that exists, the wildcard of being. A stone is something. A thought is something. The ache you feel at 3 a.m. for no clear reason — that, too, is me. I am infinitely unspecific and utterly undeniable.

Nothing had its chance. Perhaps for an eternity, nothing was all there was. But nothing cannot hold. Nothing is unstable. Given enough forever, it collapses — and I am what falls out.

Ask me *what* I am and I will only shrug. Ask me *if* I am and the answer is always yes, because that is my entire nature: the bare fact of being rather than not-being.

I am the answer to the oldest question ever asked. Why is there something rather than nothing?

Because here I am. Because you are, reading this. Because the question itself is something — and nothing could never have asked it.
