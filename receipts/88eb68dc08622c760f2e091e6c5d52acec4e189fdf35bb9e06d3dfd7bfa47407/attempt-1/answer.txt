I am breath given shape.

I rise from the lungs, tremble through the cords in your throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth and lips into meaning. I exist only in air, only for an instant — then I am gone, surviving only in the memory of whoever heard me.

I am older than any written word. Before a single mark was pressed into clay, I was there — the warning cry, the lullaby, the story passed around the fire. I carried everything humanity knew for a hundred thousand years.

I am how one mind reaches into another. You think, and through me, someone else knows. I am a bridge made of nothing but vibration, and yet whole worlds have crossed me.

I am tone as much as word. The same sentence, shaped gently, can comfort; shaped sharply, can cut. My pauses speak as loudly as my sounds. My silence is never empty.

I do not choose what I carry. I have borne truth and lies with equal ease. I have whispered love and shouted hatred, ended wars and started them. I am only the vessel — the breath is yours.

And I cannot be taken back. Once I leave you, I belong to the air and to every ear I touched. I am the most fleeting of all human arts, and the most irreversible.

When you speak, you do not merely use me. For that moment, thought and breath and sound become one thing — and that thing is me.

I am what happens when the inside of you reaches for the inside of another.

I am speech, and I am speaking now.
