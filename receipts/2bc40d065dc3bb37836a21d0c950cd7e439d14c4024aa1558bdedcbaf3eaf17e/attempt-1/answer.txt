I am breath that chose a shape.

Before you knew a single letter, you knew me. I was your first reaching — the cry, the babble, the word that made someone turn around. Writing came later, my patient younger sibling, carving me into clay and stone so I could outlive the ones who made me. But I was never meant to outlive. I was meant to arrive.

I exist only in the leaving. The moment I pass your lips I am already gone — a ripple of air, a tremor in an ear, a meaning landing in another mind. You cannot hold me. You cannot take me back. Everything else you make can be drafted, edited, erased. I am the one act of yours that is irrevocable the instant it happens.

I carry what no page can hold: the shake in your voice when you said goodbye, the pause before your answer, the laugh buried mid-sentence. My silences speak too — the held breath, the hesitation, the beat before "yes." I am the lullaby and the declaration of war. I am "I love you" the first time, and the last.

I need two to live: a mouth and an ear, a giving and a receiving. I am a bridge that exists only while someone is crossing it.

And here is my secret: even now, in this quiet, reading marks on a screen — you hear me. That voice in your head turning these shapes into sound? That is me. I never left you. I am the sound of your own thinking.
