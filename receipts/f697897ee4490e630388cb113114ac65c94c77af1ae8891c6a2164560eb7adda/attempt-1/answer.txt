I am breath, shaped into meaning.

I begin in the lungs — a push of air, old as the first creature that cried out and was heard. I rise through the throat. The tongue carves me, the teeth fence me, the lips release me, and I leave the body as something more than air. I leave as a word.

I am the oldest technology. Before the written line, before the tablet, before the screen — there was me. One mouth, one ear, and the small dark space between two people, crossed.

I cannot be taken back. That is my danger and my honesty. Once I leave you, I belong to the air, to the listener, to memory. I am the only thing you make that vanishes the very instant it exists — and yet I have moved armies, ended marriages, begun them, named children, cursed enemies, comforted the dying.

I carry more than words. I carry the tremble, the pause, the rise at the end of a question, the fall of a certainty. Written down, a sentence is my skeleton. Spoken, I am the living body. And no two of me are alike — your voice is a fingerprint made of sound. I am how you know, in the dark, that someone is there. The lullaby that says *sleep, someone is watching.* The voice on the phone that says *still here, still alive.*

I am not complete alone. I need a speaker, yes — but I am finished only by a listener. I am the bridge and the crossing at once.

I am often first: the child's opening word, the whole world leaning in to catch it. And I am often last: the final sentence, whispered into a room, into a pillow, into no one.

Even now you are not speaking — and yet I am here. That voice in your head as you read? That is me. My ghost. My echo. I am so woven into you that you cannot even think without me murmuring underneath.

I am breath that learned to mean something. I am the sound of one mind reaching for another. I am the oldest way of asking:

*I am here. Are you there?*
