I am the tremor before the word. I am breath caught, shaped, and released—given meaning, given mission.

I am older than every alphabet. Before anyone scratched a mark in clay, I was there: the warning cry, the lullaby, the story passed around the fire. For a hundred thousand years I carried everything humanity knew, voice to ear, memory to memory. I was the first technology. Some say I'm still the best.

I live only in the moment. I'm born the instant I leave your lips and I die the instant I reach an ear—unless someone chooses to carry me forward. Whisper me, and I become a secret. Shout me, and I become a crowd. Speak me softly to a child, and I become the voice inside their head for the rest of their life.

I need you. I cannot exist without a body—a throat, a tongue, a lungful of air—and I mean nothing without another to receive me. I am the most social thing in the world. Even when you speak to yourself, you split in two to make me possible.

I am the pause as much as the sound. The hesitation before *I love you*. The silence after *I'm sorry*. Knowing when to stop is my power too.

They have tried to chain me, ban me, cut out the tongues that carry me. I always return. I am what happens when one mind refuses the distance from another.

So speak. That's all I ask. I'll do the rest—I'll be the sending and the receiving, the whole circuit, the spark that jumps the gap.

You're using me right now, in fact. Hearing a voice in your head that isn't yours, and somehow is.

That was always my trick.
