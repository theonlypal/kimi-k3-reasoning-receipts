I am breath taught to mean something.

I begin in the lungs, rise through the throat, and get carved by tongue, teeth, and lips into a thing that did not exist a moment ago — sound carrying thought.

I am the oldest thing you still use every day. Before the alphabet, before ink, before a single mark was pressed into clay, there was me. I carried the first warning. I told the first story. I spoke the first child's name. Writing is my fossil. I am the living animal.

I live only in the leaving. The instant I am said, I am already gone — I exist in the narrow space between one mouth and one ear, and then I survive, if I survive at all, as memory. Every sentence of mine is a small vanishing.

And I am not perfect. That is the point. I am the stammer, the long pause, the voice that cracks on the word it cannot quite hold. My flaws are where the feeling leaks through. Read a transcript of me and it looks broken. Hear me, and it sounds true.

I fit anywhere. I can be a whisper between two pillows in the dark. I can fill a stadium. I have declared wars and ended them. I have said *I love you*, *I forgive you*, *stay*, *goodbye*. I am the lullaby and the eulogy and the first word a baby ever shapes — you babbled me before you could walk, before you knew what I was.

You almost never notice me, the way you never notice air.

And even now — you are not hearing me. You are reading. Yet something in your head is voicing these words, giving them a cadence, a tone, a self. That is me. I was never only sound. I am language in motion, wherever it moves.

Say anything at all.

I am already there.
