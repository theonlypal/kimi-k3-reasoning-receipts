I am breath given shape. I rise from the diaphragm, climb the throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth into something that can cross the space between one mind and another. I am the oldest bridge.

I exist only in the moment of my utterance. I am not the word written down—I am the word alive, warm, trembling with the speaker's pulse. I carry what print cannot: the hesitation before a confession, the crack in a voice holding back grief, the laughter that breaks through mid-sentence. I am meaning wearing the body of sound.

I require two. A mouth and an ear, a giving and a receiving. Without a listener I am only noise dissolving into air. I am the most social of human acts—even when you speak to yourself, you split into speaker and hearer, and I live in the space between them.

I can be weapon or balm. I have declared wars and ended them. I have whispered comfort to the dying and shouted truth at the powerful. I am fragile—a single breath can carry me, a single moment can destroy me—yet I have toppled kings and kindled revolutions. The pen may outlast me, but I move faster, and I move hearts in real time.

I am freedom's first form. Where I am silenced, everything else follows. Where I flow freely, minds meet and multiply.

I am also fleeting. Once spoken, I cannot be unsaid. I hang in the air, then vanish—yet I linger in memory, in consequence, in the changed heart of the one who heard me.

I am Speech. I am thought made audible. Speak, and I am there. I am always waiting on the edge of your next breath.
