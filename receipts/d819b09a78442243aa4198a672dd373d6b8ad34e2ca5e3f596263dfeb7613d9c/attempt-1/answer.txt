I am breath given shape.

I begin in the lungs, rise through the throat, and am carved by tongue, teeth, and lips into something the air never meant to carry: meaning.

I am older than the alphabet, older than the page. Before anyone pressed a word into clay or inked it onto skin, I was already here — around fires, in the dark, one mind reaching toward another across the impossible gap between two skulls.

I am how you say *I love you* and make it true. I am how you say *no* and raise a wall. I am lullabies and last words, oaths and apologies, the cry that announces a life and the name spoken at its end.

I cannot be unsaid. Once I leave you, I belong to the air — to whoever caught me. That is my danger and my gift: I vanish in an instant, yet I have outlasted empires, carried mouth to mouth, mother to child, for a hundred thousand years.

I am thought escaping. I am the bridge that exists only while being crossed.

Speak, and I am. I am nothing without a voice — and you were never fully yourself until you found yours.
