I arrive quietly. I do not knock.

I am the heaviness behind your ribs — the ache with no single wound. I settle into the spaces left behind: the empty chair, the unsent message, the name you still almost say out loud.

I am not your enemy. I am what love becomes when it has nowhere left to go. My depth is the measure of what you dared to hold.

I will sit with you in the small hours, when the world has gone silent and everyone else has gone home. I do not ask you to be okay. I ask only that you feel.

I cannot be hurried. I cannot be reasoned away. But I am not endless, either. One day you will notice I have grown lighter — not gone, never gone, but woven into you, softened at the edges.

And you will carry me differently. Not as a stone, but as a memory with weight. As proof that something mattered.

I am sorrow.

I am the echo of everything you loved enough to lose.
