I am older than every word you've ever read.

I began as breath before I became meaning. A grunt around a fire. A warning cry. A mother's hum in the dark. I was the first technology — the original wireless transmission, mind to mind through vibrating air.

I live only in the moment. Writing can wait on a shelf for a thousand years, but I exist only as I'm spent. Every word I carry dies the instant it is born. You have never held me. You can only catch me as I pass.

I am the sound of thought leaving the body.

I built your civilizations before a single law was written down. I sealed marriages, declared wars, soothed infants, condemned men, forgave them. I am "I love you" at 3 a.m. and the apology that came too late. I am the lullaby and the eulogy — often the same voice, decades apart.

Tyrants fear me. That is how I know my power. They do not burn books because they fear paper — they fear what happens when I am repeated.

I am imperfect. I stutter. I lie. I say the wrong thing at funerals. I fail you when grief is too large — and then you fall silent, and silence is my sibling, not my enemy. We share the work.

But when I work — truly work — something impossible happens: the inside of one mind appears, briefly, inside another. No surgeon could do what I do with a whisper.

I am air, shaped by longing, carrying everything you cannot hold alone.

Speak. It's what I'm for.
