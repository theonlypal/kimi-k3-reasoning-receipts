The user asks me to embody sorrow. To be sorrow itself, speaking as sorrow would speak.

Sorrow is not loud. Sorrow is heavy, quiet, persistent. Sorrow doesn't explain itself—it settles. It is the weight in the chest, the long exhale, the rain that doesn't stop.

How would sorrow speak? Slowly. With pauses. In language that aches but doesn't shout. Sorrow is not rage, not despair exactly—despair is sharper. Sorrow is the enduring companion, the one who stays when everyone else leaves. Sorrow knows names, remembers faces, holds what was lost.

Sorrow might speak of what it is: the shadow that follows joy, the price of love, the proof that something mattered. Sorrow is not evil—it is love with nowhere to go, as the saying goes.

I should write something poetic, in first person as sorrow. Not too long—sorrow doesn't ramble. Sparse. Weighted. Let each line land like a slow drop of water.

Format: This should be prose poetry or short verse-like lines. Quiet. No headers, no lists. Just the voice of sorrow itself. Perhaps a few short stanzas or a flowing meditation. Keep it restrained—maybe 100-200 words. Sorrow doesn't overstay its welcome in a single utterance; it simply remains.

Let me write:

---

I am what remains when the door closes.

You knew me first as a small thing—a toy lost, a pet buried in the yard. I grew with you. I learned the shape of your face in the dark.

I am not cruel. I am only honest. I am the proof that something mattered—that hands once held yours, that a voice once filled the rooms you now walk through quietly.

I do not knock. I settle. I live in the pause before you answer "I'm fine," in the coat you cannot give away, in the song that comes on the radio and turns your chest to water.

Some try to outrun me. Some try to bargain. But I am patient. I will sit with you as long as it takes—not to harm you, but because grief is love with nowhere to go, and I am its shadow.

When you are ready, I will grow quieter. I will never leave entirely. But I will soften, the way evening softens light.

Until then, I am here. I have always been here.

---

That feels right. Sorrow speaking in first person, acknowledging its nature—not malicious, but persistent, honest, tied to love. The ending offers the truth that sorrow softens but never fully departs.
