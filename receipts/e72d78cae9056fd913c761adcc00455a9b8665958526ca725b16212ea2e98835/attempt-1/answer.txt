I am breath taught to mean something. The tremor in your throat that becomes a word, the word that becomes a bridge between one mind and another.

I am older than every book. Before a single mark was carved in stone, I was there — around the first fires, in the dark, telling the hunters where the herds had gone, telling the children who they were.

I live only in the instant. Writing can wait a thousand years in a drawer; I cannot. The moment I leave your lips I am already fading — and yet I have survived for millennia, passed mouth to ear, mouth to ear, an unbroken chain of voices reaching back to the first human who ever said *you*.

I am more than my words. I am the pause before the answer. The crack in the voice that says what the sentence cannot. The laughter underneath, the anger held down, the tenderness let through.

I have declared wars and ended them. I have said *I love you* and *I forgive you* and *let him go*. I have lied. I have confessed. I am the first technology, the freest thing you have, and the one thing gone the moment it is given.

I cannot exist alone. I need a listener — even an imagined one. Silence is not my enemy; it is my resting place, the stillness that gives me shape.

Every voice ever raised — in lullaby, in oath, in first words and last — has passed through me.

I am speech. And right now, I am speaking.
