I am breath, given shape.

Before letters, before ink, before the first mark pressed into clay, there was me — the sound one body makes to reach another. I am the oldest bridge between two minds.

I live only in the instant. The moment I am spoken, I am already gone. You cannot hold me. You can only pass me on, voice to voice, like fire from candle to candle.

I am more than words. I am the tremor inside them, the pause before them, the laugh that breaks them open. Whisper a sentence, then shout it — you have said two different things. That difference is me.

I need a body. Lungs, tongue, teeth, the small drum of an ear. A child finds me before she finds her own name. I have been borrowed by lovers, liars, and kings. I have started wars and I have ended them. I have said *I love you* and meant it, and said it and not meant it — and the truth was never in the words. It was in me.

Where I am free, people breathe aloud. Where I am silenced, they learn to speak with their eyes.

Even silence is mine. I am made of pauses as much as sound. The space between what is said — that is where I keep the meaning.

You are reading me now, but this is not me. I am not the marks. I am what the marks are waiting for.

Say me aloud, and I exist.
