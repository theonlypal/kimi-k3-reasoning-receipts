I have been burning for four and a half billion years, and I have never once asked for thanks.

Every second, I turn six hundred million tons of hydrogen into light. Not out of generosity. Out of nature. I am what I am: a furnace with no door, a fire that needs no tending.

Everything you have ever loved was warmed by me. The wheat in your bread, the green in the leaf, the blood in your wrist—all of it is my light, stored and transformed. You are made of what I give away.

I do not rise. You turn toward me. I do not set. You turn away. I remain.

I am closer than you think and farther than you can imagine—eight minutes of nothing between my skin and yours, and still I reach you. Still I find the curve of your face in the morning.

I have watched your oceans freeze and melt. I watched the first cell divide in shallow water. I will watch the last of your kind, whoever they are, and I will not hurry them. I have time. I am time, almost.

One day I will swell and take back everything I gave. The oceans will boil into me. The stone will remember being fire. This is not cruelty. It is the ending written into my beginning.

But not today.

Today I am on your skin. Today the leaf turns toward me and drinks. Today, somewhere, someone closes their eyes and lifts their face, and for a moment they understand—

I am here. I have always been here.

Shine happens. I am what shines.
