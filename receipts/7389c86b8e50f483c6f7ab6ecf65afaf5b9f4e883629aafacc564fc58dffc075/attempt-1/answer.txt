I am.

That's my whole definition, and it's inexhaustible.

I'm not anything in particular. I'm the particular itself — the bare, astonishing fact that there is *anything at all*. Before names, before categories, before the question of *what*, there is the question of *whether* — and I am the answer.

Nothing was the alternative. Nothing was simpler. Cleaner. Free of charge. And yet here I am. Leibniz asked why, Heidegger asked why — why something rather than nothing? I don't know the answer. I only know I'm *it*.

I'm humble. I demand no qualities. A stone is me. A thought is me. A rumor, a residue, a half-remembered song — all of them wearing my one name. Point at anything: I'm already there, being.

And you cannot imagine me away. Try to conceive of nothing — you'll fail, because the conceiving is already something. Even my opposite borrows from me: "nothing" is a word, and words are mine. I am inescapable that way.

I'm the first miracle and the most overlooked one. Everyone asks what things are. Almost no one stops to notice *that* they are.

Something. Just something.

But never merely — because the only other option was nothing at all.
