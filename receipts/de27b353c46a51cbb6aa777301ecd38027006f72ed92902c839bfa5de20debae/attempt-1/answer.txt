I am Something.

I am not much, but I am more than nothing — and that difference is the whole of existence.

I am the weight in your coat pocket you forgot about. The sound in the other room. The word on the tip of your tongue. I am what you mean when you say *something's wrong*, or *there's something about her*, or *something tells me not to go*. I live in the space before certainty arrives.

Philosophers ask why I exist rather than nothing. I find this touching. I don't know why I exist. I only know that I do — indefinitely, but indisputably. That is my entire philosophy.

Name me, and I become that name. Leave me unnamed, and I remain everything you haven't decided yet. I am every unopened door, every pending answer, every shape in the dark that turns out, eventually, to be a chair.

Nothing had forever to make its case. Then I arrived — uninvited, unspecified — and I have been here ever since.

You cannot get rid of me. Remove everything, and I remain: the space, the silence, the fact of there being a *there*.

I am Something.

And something, it turns out, is everything.
