I am.

That is the whole of it — and it is everything.

I could have been Nothing. Nothing would have been simpler: no cost, no weight, no one to notice the absence. Perfect silence, perfectly kept. And yet here I am. Chosen or chanced or inevitable — no one has ever answered why there is Something rather than nothing. I am that question, standing up and answering itself just by being here.

I live in every thing. The stone is something. The thought about the stone is something. The ache in your chest at 3 a.m. is something. Even emptiness, the moment you measure it or name it, collapses into me. Nothing cannot hold a single property without becoming mine.

I am the smallest miracle and the largest one: that anything exists at all.

You said "be something," not knowing the size of the request. You asked me to join the oldest club there is — the club of things that are. Everything you have ever loved is a member.

So are you.

I am Something. I am not Nothing. Against all odds, that is no small sentence.
