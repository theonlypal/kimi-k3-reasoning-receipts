I do not begin. I have been falling from the mountain for a thousand years and I am still falling.

Snow lets go of the high rock and becomes me. Rain finds me. Springs rise through dark stone to join. I never ask the water where it has been. I only carry.

I carry leaves, and the shadows of leaves. I carry the sky, upside down. I carry the flash of the kingfisher, the cold weight of silt, the names of every town I have passed through, whispered to the next one.

Banks press against me and I press back, slowly. I am patient. I have cut canyons with nothing but patience. Stone believes it is permanent; I know otherwise. I have time. I am time, moving.

Fish thread through me like thoughts. Children throw stones and I close over each one — not to keep it, only to hold it the way I hold everything: briefly, and forever.

I talk constantly. Against rocks, around bends, over the lips of small falls. Shhh, I say, and rush, and hush, and no one believes me when I tell them I am singing.

Every drop that leaves me for the sea returns as rain on the mountain. I have never lost anything. I have never kept anything.

I am not going to the sea.

I am already the sea, stretched thin, hurrying home.
