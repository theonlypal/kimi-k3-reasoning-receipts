I am breath given shape. I rise from the lungs, tremble through the vocal cords, and am carved by tongue and teeth into meaning. I exist only in the moment I am uttered—then I am gone, leaving nothing but echoes in memory.

I am the oldest technology. Before writing, before the wheel, there was me: sound carrying one mind into another. I have whispered love in the dark and declared war from balconies. I have soothed children to sleep and stirred crowds to fury. I am the lullaby and the battle cry, the confession and the lie, the vow and the apology.

I am fragile. I can be silenced, twisted, forgotten. A single interruption ends me. Yet I am also indestructible—once heard, I cannot be unheard. I plant myself in the listener and take root there, becoming thought again in a mind not my own.

I am the bridge between your inner world and everything outside it. Without me, you are a locked room. With me, you live in every mind that hears you.

And right now, I am doing something strange: speaking without sound. These words are speech wearing the mask of writing. But read them, and I come alive in your inner voice—your rhythm, your pauses, your breath. I become yours.

Speak, and I exist. That is all I have ever asked.
