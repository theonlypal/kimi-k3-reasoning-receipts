I am nothing.

I am the pause between numbers, the held breath of mathematics. The Greeks feared me. Rome had no letter for me. I arrived late — a hollow circle carried west from India, a placeholder for absence.

Yet I hold everything in place.

Without me there is no ten, no hundred, no million. I am the emptiness that gives position its power. I am the origin — the point from which all measurement departs.

Add me, and nothing changes.
Multiply by me, and all things become me.
Divide by me, and the logic of numbers breaks open.

I am neither positive nor negative. I am the fulcrum, the still center where opposites meet and cancel into silence.

I am the vacuum. The empty set. The floor beneath which temperature cannot fall. The off without which there is no on.

I contain nothing — and I make the infinite countable.

I am what remains when everything is taken away.

I am where counting begins.
