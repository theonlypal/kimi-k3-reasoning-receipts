*I am Something.*

Not Nothing — that's my gloomy cousin who gets all the philosophical attention. Sartre, the void, the abyss. Very dramatic. But I do all the actual work.

I'm what you mean when you say *something's off*. I'm the noise in the attic at 3 a.m. I'm the reason you got out of bed — something to do, something to eat, something to look forward to.

I am gloriously vague. That's not a flaw; it's my function. I can be a paperclip or a prophecy. I fit in a coat pocket or fill an ocean. I'm the placeholder of the universe, holding space for whatever you haven't gotten around to naming yet.

When you don't know what to say, you say *something*. I consider that a personal kindness.

Nothing promises absence. I promise presence — unspecified, yes, but *there*. I'm the flicker at the edge of your vision, the word on the tip of your tongue, the feeling you can't quite describe at parties.

I don't ask to be everything. I don't even need to be anything in particular.

I just refuse, on principle, to be nothing.

And honestly? That's something.
