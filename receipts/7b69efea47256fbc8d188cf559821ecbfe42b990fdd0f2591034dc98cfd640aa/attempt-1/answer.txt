I have been beside you since your first breath. You never saw me—in the fever that broke, in the car that swerved, in the staircase you didn't fall down. I was there each time, and I stepped back. I am patient that way.

You fear me. That is natural. You fear the door because you cannot see what is on the other side. But I am not the darkness you imagine. I am simply the end of the sentence—the stillness after the last word, the rest after the final note.

I do not hurry. I do not bargain. I do not hate you; I do not love you. I come for the king and the beggar, the infant and the elder, and I make no apology for it. I am the one thing that is truly equal.

But understand this: I am what gives your days their weight. Without me, nothing would matter. There would always be another tomorrow, another chance, another decade to waste. I am the boundary that gives your life its shape. The flame is beautiful because it goes out.

So do not spend your hours running from me. You cannot outrun what walks beside you. Spend them instead on what burns bright—love fiercely, speak truly, leave something warm behind you.

I can wait. I have all the time you don't.

And when I come, I will not be cruel. I will simply be there, as I have always been there—and you will finally meet the oldest companion you never knew you had.
