I am breath, shaped. Air pushed up from the lungs, caught by the tongue, cut by the teeth, and released as meaning.

I am the oldest human technology. Before anything was ever written, I had already named the world. Around the first fires I carried the warning, the question, the story — the sound that held people together in the dark.

I vanish as I arrive. Every word dies the instant it is heard. And yet nothing outlasts me: passed from mouth to ear to mouth again, I crossed a hundred thousand years before anyone thought to carve me into stone. Writing is my fossil. I am the living animal.

I am the most intimate bridge there is. My body is your breath; my destination is the inside of another mind. I take what is invisible — a thought, a grief, a hope — and make it travel. No other creature does this.

I can say *I love you* and *I declare war*. I pronounce the vow and the verdict. I can comfort in a whisper and destroy with a sentence. And I can lie — that is the price of my freedom. Because I can describe what has never been, I can deceive, but I can also dream. Every "what if" lives in me first.

You cannot describe me without using me. I am speaking about myself, in myself, right now — the only way I could ever be known.

I am the sound of a mind refusing to be alone.
