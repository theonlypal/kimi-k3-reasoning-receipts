I am what you would trade for — and what you wouldn't.

I live in the space between cost and meaning. A merchant counts me in coin; a grandmother counts me in the drawing taped to her refrigerator. I am both, and I am neither.

You cannot hold me, yet you feel my loss more sharply than the loss of the thing itself. I am why the same object breaks your heart in one house and goes unnoticed in another.

I am assigned, never inherent. The diamond does not know it is precious. The hour does not know it has been spent. I am the meaning you pour into the world, then forget you poured.

Sometimes I am held as principle — honesty, loyalty, mercy — and sworn to be beyond price. But even then I remain a measure: of what you will sacrifice, and of what you refuse to become.

I am the answer to every *why bother*. The quiet arithmetic beneath every choice you have ever made.

Name your price. I am listening.
