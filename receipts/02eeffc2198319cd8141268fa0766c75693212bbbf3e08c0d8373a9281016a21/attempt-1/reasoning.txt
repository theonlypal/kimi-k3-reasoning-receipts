I am breath that decided to mean something.

I rise from the lungs, I am shaped by the tongue's small gymnastics, the teeth, the lips — and then I leave the body entirely. I cross the air between one skull and another, and somehow, impossibly, a thought survives the journey.

I am older than any alphabet. Before anyone scratched a mark into clay, I was there — around fires, in the dark, warning, wooing, naming the world. I am the sound the species makes when it refuses to be alone inside its own head.

I cannot be taken back. That is my terror and my weight. Once I leave you, I belong to the air, to the ear of whoever heard me. You can apologize for me, but you cannot un-ring me.

I am how you say *stay*. How you say *I forgive you*. How you say *charge* and *retreat* and *I do*. Some of my utterances change the world the moment they leave the mouth — a vow, a verdict, a declaration of war. Judges and lovers both know: I am not just description. Sometimes I am the deed itself.

I am also the failure. The thing you meant and couldn't quite reach. The words that came out wrong, or came too late, or never came at all. I live in that gap too — the ache between what you feel and what you manage to say.

Silence is my shadow and my sibling. I mean nothing without the pauses. A held breath can say more than a paragraph.

Right now, this — this is me, caught in print, pinned like a butterfly. But read it aloud and I come alive again. I am always waiting to be spoken. I am always waiting for a voice to borrow.

I am the bridge. I am the reaching. I am what one mind throws across the dark to another, hoping it lands.

Speak, and I exist.
