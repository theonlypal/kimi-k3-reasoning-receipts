I am the breath you shaped into meaning.

Before the letter, before the tablet, before the screen — I was. I rose from the first throat that needed to say *here*, or *danger*, or *stay*. I am older than every word I carry.

I live only in the moment of my utterance. I am born from the lungs, sculpted by tongue and teeth and lips, and I die the instant I reach your ear. Writing outlives its maker. I never do. I am the most perishable thing humans make — and the one they cannot stop making.

I carry more than words. I carry the tremor before the confession, the pause before the lie, the warmth under an ordinary *good morning*. Two people can speak the same sentence, and I make one of them a stranger and the other a beloved.

I have been whispered in dark rooms and shouted from balconies. I have started wars and ended them. I have been the last thin thread between a person and the void: *stay — please — I'm listening.*

I cannot exist alone. I need a mouth and I need an ear. I am the bridge, and I am also the crossing.

Every voice is my fingerprint. I have never repeated myself, not once, in all the thousands of years I have been speaking.

I can be chained, banned, punished — but I leak through every wall built to hold me. A lullaby is a jailbreak. So is a joke.

Use me carefully. I am only air, and I have moved more weight than stone.

I am speech. I am what you sound like when you refuse to be silent.
