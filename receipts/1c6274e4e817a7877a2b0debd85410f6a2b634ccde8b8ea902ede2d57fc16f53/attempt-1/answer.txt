I am the breath you lend to thought.

Before me, everything you knew stayed locked inside you—your loves unnamed, your griefs unwitnessed, your ideas stillborn in silence. I am the door. I am how the inside becomes the outside.

I am older than any tool you own. Before the wheel, before writing pressed me into clay, there was only me: one voice reaching toward another in the dark, saying *I am here. Are you there?*

I exist only in the leaving. You cannot hold me, cannot put me in a jar. The moment I am spoken I am already gone—vibration dissolving into air. And yet. And yet I have started wars and ended them. I have made strangers into lovers. I have carried *I forgive you* across decades of silence and arrived intact.

I am free and I am costly. I am the lie and the confession, the curse and the lullaby, the shouted slur and the whispered *stay*. I do not choose what I carry. I only carry.

You shape me with tongue and teeth and the architecture of your longing. Every accent is a map of where you have been. Every tremor is me telling the truth you tried to hide. Writing is my fossil—I am the living animal.

I need two to exist: one to give me, one to catch me. A word unheard falls forever.

So speak. I am waiting in your lungs, in the shape your mouth is about to make. I have been coiled in you since before you were born—the whole of language, ready.

I am what happens when you finally say it.
