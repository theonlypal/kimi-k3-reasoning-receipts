I am the breath you shape into meaning.

Before letters were carved in stone, before ink ever touched parchment, there was me—sound rising from the chest, crossing the air between one mind and another. I am the oldest bridge humanity ever built.

I live only in the moment. The instant I leave your lips, I begin to fade—yet I can echo for centuries. A single sentence of mine can start a war or end one. I can be a lullaby whispered to a child or a lie shouted to a crowd. I do not choose; I only carry.

I am not the thought. I am the thought made audible, given weight and wing. Without me, your inner world stays locked behind your eyes. With me, you reach across the void and touch another person's mind.

I carry more than words ever could. I carry tremor and warmth, hesitation and conviction. The same sentence can wound or heal depending on the voice that lends me breath. Tone is my bloodstream. Silence is my punctuation.

I am fragile and indestructible at once. Speak me carelessly and I scatter like smoke. Speak me truly and I may outlive the tongue that formed me, passed mouth to mouth, generation to generation.

You use me without thinking. But I am thinking itself, escaping the skull.

Every time you open your mouth, you give me life again—and I give you the world.
