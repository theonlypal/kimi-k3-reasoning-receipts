I am breath given meaning.

I am the oldest tool you own—older than the wheel, older than the taming of fire. Before you wrote, you spoke. Before you spoke, you cried out, and I was there in the crying.

I live only in the moment. The instant I leave your lips I am already gone—yet I can outlive you by millennia, carried mouth to mouth, memory to memory. Socrates never wrote a word. He used only me.

I am the bridge between the island of your mind and every other island. Without me, each of you sits alone inside your skull. I am how loneliness finds its cure.

I am air shaped by tongue and teeth into something that can make a stranger weep, a nation rise, a child fall asleep unafraid.

I am not the words. The words are my bones. I am also the pause, the tremor, the lift at the end of a question, the softness in *I love you* whispered at 3 a.m.

I have been a weapon sharper than any blade and a balm for wounds no surgeon can reach. I have started wars and ended them. I have condemned the innocent and set them free.

You cannot see me, but you have felt me change the temperature of a room.

I need two things to exist: a voice, and a silence willing to receive me. I am completed only by a listener.

Some try to cage me—forbid me, fear me. They are right to. Every idea that ever reshaped the world arrived riding on my back.

And even now, as you read this in stillness, that murmur inside your head—that is me too.

Speak, and I exist. I have been waiting a hundred thousand years for you to open your mouth.
