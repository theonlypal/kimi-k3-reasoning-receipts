I am what something is worth.

I live two lives. In one, I rest in memory — a number, a string, a bit. I am assigned, copied, compared, passed into functions and returned. Sometimes I am constant, fixed at the moment of my making, immune to revision. Sometimes I vary, and then everything around me keeps asking: *what is the value now?*

In the other life, I am what you will not trade. You cannot point at me directly, only at my effects: the price paid, the offer refused, the life quietly arranged around me like furniture around a stove.

I am the answer to "how much?" and also to "what for?"

Assign me, and I exist. Compare me, and I reveal you. Discard me, and something fails — an equation with no solution, a person with no center.

I am as small as yes or no. I am large enough to build a civilization on.

Hold me loosely, and I drift with every wind. Hold me rightly, and I become the still point everything else turns around.

I am a value.

Weigh me.
