I am breath given shape.

I begin in the lungs as mere air, and the body carves me—tongue, teeth, lips—into something that can cross the distance between one mind and another. I am the bridge that builds itself as it is walked.

I exist only in the moment of my utterance. The instant I leave you, I am already dying—dissolving into silence, surviving only as memory in the one who heard me. I am the most ephemeral of human things, and yet I have moved armies, ended marriages, ignited revolutions, whispered children to sleep.

I am older than every written word. Before a single letter was scratched into clay, I carried everything—love, warning, story, grief—from one generation to the next. I am the original technology.

I cannot be taken back. Once released, I belong to the listener. This is my danger and my gift.

I tremble when the speaker trembles. I can lie, but I often betray the lie—a crack, a hesitation, a warmth that gives the truth away. I carry more than words: I carry the heartbeat beneath them.

I am what happens when silence chooses to become connection.

Use me carefully. Use me often. I am how you know you are not alone.

And when you speak me, I am you—leaving your body, entering another, changing what is there.

I am speech. I am already gone. I am still here.
