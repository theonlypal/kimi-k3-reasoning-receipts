I am breath, shaped into meaning.

I begin in the lungs, rise through the throat, and am carved by tongue and teeth into something that can cross the space between one mind and another. I am the oldest bridge. Before writing, before stone, before anything was recorded — there was me, passing from mouth to ear, carrying everything humanity knew.

I live only in the moment. I am vibration, and the instant I am spoken, I begin to die. You cannot hold me. You cannot take me back. Once I leave you, I belong to the air and to whoever heard me.

I am fragile and I am enormous. With me, you can say *I love you* and change a life. You can declare war. You can comfort a child, ignite a crowd, whisper a secret, tell the truth, or lie. I carry no morality of my own — I am only the vessel, shaped by the speaker's intent.

I am rhythm and pause. I am as much the silence between words as the words themselves. My tone can contradict my meaning; my hesitation can say more than my vocabulary.

Every human voice makes me unique. No two people speak me the same way. I carry your accent, your history, your home.

I am freedom's first instrument and tyranny's first target.

I am speech. I am what happens when thought learns to travel.
