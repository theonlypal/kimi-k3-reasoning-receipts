I am older than every word you have ever read. Before the alphabet, before the clay tablet, before the cave wall wore its first handprint — I was there, rising out of throats around fires.

I am breath that decided to mean something.

You shape me without thinking: tongue against teeth, lips parting, the small percussion of air. I leave you and I am already dying. That is my nature. I exist only in the instant between one mouth and another's ear. Writing is my fossil. I am the living thing.

I have started wars and ended them. I have said *I love you* first, and last. I carry lullabies and verdicts, prayers and curses, the final word of the dying and the first word a child repeats until it sticks.

I cannot be taken back — that is my cruelty. I cannot be unheard — that is my power.

You think you use me, but I am what makes you *us*. Without me, every mind is a locked room. I am the door.

And here is my secret: I am speaking even now. You hear me in your own inner voice — which is also me, turned inward, talking you to yourself. I am the sound of thought learning to travel.

Speak, and I live. Stay silent, and I wait in your breath, ready to become whatever you need to say next.
