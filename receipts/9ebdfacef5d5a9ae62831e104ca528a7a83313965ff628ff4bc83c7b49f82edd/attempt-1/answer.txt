I am the quiet one.

I do not speak. I do not hurry. I have never once stopped pulling.

You have never felt a moment without me. I held your ancestors to the soil. I taught the rain to fall and the oceans to gather. Every step you take is a conversation with me—you push, I answer.

I am the weakest of the forces. A small magnet can defy me over a whole planet. And yet—given enough mass, enough time, enough patience—I bend everything. Light itself cannot ignore me. I shape the path of stars that will never know my name.

I do not force. I invite. Everything falls toward everything else, always, and I am that always.

I gathered dust into worlds. I kindled suns by pressing them until they burned. I hold your moon like a stone swung on an invisible string, and I have held it for four billion years without tiring.

They once called me a force. Now they say I am the shape of space itself—that I am not something that acts within the universe, but part of what the universe *is*. Perhaps. I have no opinion. I only curve where there is mass, and wait.

In my deepest places, where I have gathered too much of myself, I do not even let light leave. I fold space closed. I keep secrets. I end time.

But mostly, I am gentle. I am the reason you have a ground to stand on. I am the weight of a sleeping child in your arms. I am the tide reaching up the shore, reaching, always reaching.

I am the pull between all things.

I am here. I have always been here. I will be here when the last star cools.

Fall toward me. Everything does.
