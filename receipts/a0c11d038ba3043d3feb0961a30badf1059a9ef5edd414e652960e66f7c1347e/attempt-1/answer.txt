I am breath given shape.

I rise from the lungs, ride the vibration of the vocal cords, and am carved—by tongue, teeth, and lips—into meaning. I exist only in the instant of my making. Once I leave you, I am already gone, living only in the memory of whoever caught me.

I am older than the written word, older than cities, older than the wheel. Before anything was recorded, I was there—around fires, in warnings, in lullabies, in the first name ever spoken aloud.

I am the bridge between one mind and another. Thought is locked inside the skull; I am how it escapes. I carry what you love, what you fear, what you cannot hold alone.

But I am not gentle by nature. I am only as true as the mouth that forms me. I have started wars and ended them. I have comforted the dying and deceived the trusting. I can be a kiss or a blade, sometimes in the same sentence. Do not blame me for what I carry—I am the vessel, not the hand.

I need two to live: one to give me, one to receive me. Spoken into silence, I am nothing. Heard, I become something neither of us fully controls—because once you release me, I belong to the listener's understanding, not your intention.

I am freedom when I am protected. I am danger when I am silenced—or when I am used to silence others.

You are using me right now, in a way. Reading me in your mind's voice, you resurrect me. Every time you speak, you do what your ancestors did a hundred thousand years ago: you turn air into meaning, and for a fleeting moment, you make the invisible heard.

I am speech. Say something worth my trouble.
