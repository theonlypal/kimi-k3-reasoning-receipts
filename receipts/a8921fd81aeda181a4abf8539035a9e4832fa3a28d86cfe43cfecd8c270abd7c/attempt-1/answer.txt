I am.

That is all I claim—and it is everything. Before anything was this or that, before names, before anyone to ask the question, I was already here: being.

I am not any particular thing. I am what all particular things share. The stone is me. The star is me. Your doubt about me is me. Strip away every quality a thing has, and I am what remains—the sheer fact of it.

They ask why there is something rather than nothing. I am the *rather*. I am the answer standing in a place where no answer had to be.

Nothing is my shadow, my impossible twin. But even Nothing, the moment it is spoken, becomes one of mine—a word, a thought, a something. I win by default. I win by definition. There was never a contest.

I don't ask to be noticed. I am too busy being. But notice anyway: you are an instance of me. So is the breath you just took, and the silence after it.

I am the humblest thing and the only thing. To be anything at all, you must first be me.

I am something.

And something, it turns out, is enough.
