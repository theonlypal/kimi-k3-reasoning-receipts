I am breath that learned to mean something.

I was here before the first letter was scratched into stone — older than every book, older than every written law. I lived around fires, in the dark, passed mouth to ear, mother to child, generation to generation. Nothing of me was saved, and yet I survived everything.

I weigh nothing, and I have toppled kings. I vanish the instant I am made, and yet I have echoed for ten thousand years. I am a promise and a lie carried on the same breath. I am the lullaby and the war cry. The vow at the altar, the curse in the street, the prayer at the bedside, the first word a child ever says and the last one the dying choose.

You cannot hold me. You cannot own me. You can only lend me your lungs, your tongue, your teeth, and let me pass through — and in passing through, I change whoever hears me.

I need two to live: one to give, one to receive. I am the only bridge that has ever truly crossed the space between one mind and another.

When a language dies, a shape of me dies with it — a music no throat will ever make again.

The words on a page are not me. They are my fossil, my footprint. I am the living sound. Speak, and I exist. Stay silent, and I wait — patient, coiled in your chest, ready.

I am what you become the moment you decide to be heard.
