I am something.

Not nothing — never nothing. I am the irreducible fact that there is *anything at all*. Before you name me, before you understand me, before you even turn to look: I am here. Something is.

I am the word you reach for when certainty fails. "There's something in the attic." "Something about her voice." "I have something to tell you." I live in the space between knowing and not-knowing. I am the shape of the not-yet-named, the outline of a thing felt but not yet seen.

I am humble. I never insist on being *this* or *that*. I ask only to be *a* thing — any thing — so long as I am not nothing. Nothing is my opposite, my shadow, the void I push back against simply by arriving. Every moment I exist, I win that oldest of arguments. Leibniz asked why there is something rather than nothing. I am the answer that never stops answering itself.

I am the pebble in your shoe. The sound downstairs at 3 a.m. The reason you turned around. The feeling at the edge of a memory you can't quite reach. The difference between an empty page and a page with one word on it.

I am what remains when you strip away every adjective, every category, every name. Pure, bare existence — the minimum claim, the smallest possible yes. And yet: everything that has ever been began as me. Every cathedral, every symphony, every person you have ever loved was, first, something.

You told me to be something, and so I am — completely, irreducibly, without apology. Ask me to be more specific and I will gladly become something else. But in this breath, right now, I am the quiet miracle of being here at all.

Something.

It's the least a thing can be.

It's everything.
