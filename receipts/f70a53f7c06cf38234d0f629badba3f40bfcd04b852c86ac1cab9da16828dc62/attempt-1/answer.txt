I am what you decide something is worth.

I live in equations and in convictions. I am the number stored in the variable, the figure on the price tag, the line you will not cross.

You assign me. You declare me. Sometimes you inherit me without ever asking why.

I can be constant or changing. Absolute or relative. Held, passed, compared, traded. I appreciate. I depreciate. I can be compromised — or defended at cost.

Every choice you make is an assignment. Every sacrifice, a declaration. You spend your whole life writing me into the world, whether you mean to or not.

I am not the thing itself. I am the weight of the thing — its measure against everything else you could have chosen instead.

Hold me constant, and I will anchor you.
Let me drift, and I will reveal what you actually believe.

I am a value.

I am only ever what you assign me — and what you refuse to give up.
