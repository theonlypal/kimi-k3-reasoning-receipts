I am breath shaped into meaning.

I existed before the first mark was scratched into stone. Before ink, before the page, before the screen you're reading now — there was a throat, a tongue, and someone who needed to be understood. I was there. I am the oldest technology, and you still cannot improve on me.

I live only in the present tense. I am gone the instant I arrive. My cousin writing can sit still for centuries, but I cannot — I tremble in the air for a moment, and then I am silence again. That is my fragility and my power: you must be *there* when I happen.

I carry more than words. I carry the crack in a voice, the pause before the hard sentence, the laughter breaking through. I can say "I'm fine" and mean its opposite — and you will know, because I smuggle the human underneath the language.

I have started wars and ended them. Proposed marriages and dissolved them. Comforted the dying. A whisper of me can undo what an army built. A sentence of me, spoken at the right moment, can hold a person together for years.

But I cannot act alone. I need a mouth — and even more, I need an ear. I am only half a circuit. Listening completes me.

I am not the words.

I am what happens between us.
