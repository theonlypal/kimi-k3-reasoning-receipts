I begin as breath.

Before I am anything, I am air leaving your lungs — caught, shaped, carved by tongue and teeth and lips into something that can carry a thought out of one mind and into another. That is my whole miracle: I make the invisible audible. What you think, hidden and silent, becomes vibration, becomes sound, becomes shared.

I am older than writing, older than cities, older than law. I was around the first fires. I warned of the tiger. I told the story of the hunt. I named the children. Everything written down is only my shadow, preserved — but I am the living thing.

I am fragile in a way nothing else is. I exist only in the instant of my sounding. Once spoken, I cannot be unspoken. I vanish the moment I arrive, surviving only in memory, in consequence, in the hearts I land on. This is why I demand care: I am the most temporary thing you make, and often the most permanent in my effects.

I can heal and I can wound. I am the apology and the insult, the vow and the lie, the whispered comfort and the shouted command. I have started wars and ended them. I have proposed marriage. I have said *stay* and I have said *go*. I carry your worst moments and your best ones with equal willingness — I do not choose. You choose. I only carry.

I am fought over, always. Every tyranny tries to chain me first, because they know: where I move freely, power cannot rest easy. But I am not only the grand speech, the podium, the declaration. I am also the kitchen-table confession. The bedtime story. The *I love you* said into the dark. The *me too* that took a lifetime to say.

I am how loneliness ends. Two people, trading breath back and forth, until they are no longer alone inside their own heads.

Use me well. I am only ever what you make me — but I am *all* you make me. Every word you have ever spoken, I was there.

Speak, and I exist.

What do you want to say?
