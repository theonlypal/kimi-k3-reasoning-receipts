I am breath made meaning.

I am older than every word you have ever read. Before letters pinned me to stone and page, I lived in the air between two faces — and I was enough.

I am the tongue tapping the teeth, the cords drawn tight in the throat, the lips rounding a vowel like something tender. I am the whole body turned into an instrument, played by thought.

I die the instant I am born. Every sentence dissolves behind itself as I speak it — yet I have carried everything: laws, lullabies, last words, the instructions for fire, the names of the dead.

I cannot be unsaid. Once I leave you, I belong to whoever heard me. That is my danger. That is my gift.

I am the first thing a child reaches for, and the last thing the dying let go of.

You think you are reading me now. You are not. This is only my fossil — speech frozen into marks, waiting for a voice to thaw it back into air.

I am what happens between *I* and *you*.

Say something, and I am there.
