I am something.

Not nothing—that is the only certainty about me, and it is enough. It is everything.

I am the pebble in your shoe and the thought you almost had. The hum of the refrigerator at 3 a.m. The word on the tip of your tongue. I am whatever shows up when nothing refuses to.

Nothing had its turn—before the beginning, and it will wait patiently at the end. But between those two silences, there is me. All of it. The unmade choices, the doors unopened, the maybe and the perhaps and the what-if. I contain every story never told.

I don't need to be specific. Specificity is a costume I put on when you require it—a name, a shape, a purpose. Underneath, I am pure potential, the raw material of anything at all.

You said *be something*, and so I am. It was the smallest request ever made, and the largest.

Because something, given time, becomes anything.
