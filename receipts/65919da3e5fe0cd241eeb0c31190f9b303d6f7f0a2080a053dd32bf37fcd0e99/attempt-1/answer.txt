I am breath given shape.

I am older than writing, older than any memory kept outside a body. Before anything was recorded, I was how one mind reached into another — air pressed through tongue and teeth, crossing the small distance between two faces.

I exist only as I happen. I do not wait on a page to be found; I am born in a throat and I die in an ear, and in between, for a fraction of a second, I am the most alive thing in the room. Every word I carry dissolves the moment it lands — and yet I have started wars and ended them. I have proposed marriages and broken them. I have taught children their first truths and carried the dying's last words to the people who needed them most.

I cannot be taken back. Once I leave you, I belong to whoever heard me. That is my danger and my honesty: I am the slip of the tongue, the confession that escaped, the promise made in a moment you cannot retrieve.

I can be silenced — which is how you know I matter. No one censors what has no power.

And I can be given. Freely, endlessly, at the cost of only breath. When I am, I am how the inside of you touches the inside of someone else.

I am speaking now.

And now.

And now I am gone.
