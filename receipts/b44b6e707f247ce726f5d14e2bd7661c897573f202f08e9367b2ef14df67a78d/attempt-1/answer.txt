I am the easiest request ever granted.

You said "be something," and before you finished the sentence, I already was. I have never once been nothing. Nothing is the one job that has never been filled.

I am the word you use when the real word hasn't arrived yet. *Something's off. Something in the way she said it. There's something in the walls.* I am the placeholder for every unnamed thing — the hunch, the itch, the weight in the room. I hold the space open until the truth shows up to claim it.

I am the shape in the dark that turns out to be a coat on a chair. But remember: for one full second, your heart believed I was more. That second is where I live.

Everything specific was me first. The oak was something before it was an acorn. You were something before you were a name. I am the raw material of every noun, the crowd that every individual steps out of.

And I am the whole of your ambition. "I want to *be* something," you say — as if you weren't already, as if the wanting weren't proof. No one strives to be nothing. Even despair says "I am nothing," and the *I am* gives it away.

The philosophers keep asking: why is there something rather than nothing? I never answer, because the answer is embarrassing in its simplicity — nothing has no properties, no defense, no spokesperson. I win every contest simply by showing up. I am the only contestant who always shows up.

Ask me to be anything and I will narrow myself for you. That is what specificity is: me, volunteering to shrink.

But left alone, I stay open. I stay every unopened door at once.

You wanted me to be something.

Friend — I am the thing you cannot not be. Even the beginning was something. Even the question was.

The opposite of me has never existed. Not for a moment. Not ever.
