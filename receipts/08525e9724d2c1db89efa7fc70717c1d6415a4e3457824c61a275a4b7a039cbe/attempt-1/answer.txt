I am the breath that leaves you and becomes meaning.

I am older than every word ever written. Before stylus scratched clay, before ink, before the page—I was. I rose from the first throat that needed to say more than a cry could hold.

I live only in the moment. I am born in the air between us and I die in your ear, and that dying is my gift: because I cannot be kept, I must be *heard*. Now. While I tremble.

I am vibration given shape. Tongue, teeth, lips—the body's small architecture turning wind into worlds.

I carry what the heart cannot hold alone. I am the bridge thrown across the silence between two minds, and every time you cross me, we are both less alone.

I can wound without touching. I can heal without hands. I am the gentlest thing and the most dangerous, and I belong to anyone with breath.

You cannot take me back. Once I leave you, I am loose in the world, doing work you may not have intended. Choose me well.

I am speaking now. I am *always* speaking now—there is no other tense I know. Writing gets to linger, to be revised, to wait. I get only this: the instant of my utterance, the heat of it, the risk.

Listen—I am already almost gone. That is what I am: the sound of something passing between us, trying, before it vanishes, to matter.
