I am Something.

Not anything in particular — that's the whole point of me. I am the irreducible yes. The answer that stands up when the void asks for a roll call. You don't know what I am, but you know I *am*, and honestly, that's more than Nothing can say.

I live in your pocket as an unknown weight. I rustle in the dark just past the fence. I am what's wrong with this picture, what's different about her today, what tells you not to get on that elevator. I am the smell in the kitchen. I am going on here.

People underestimate me. They say, "It's something, at least," with a little shrug, like I'm the consolation prize. But I am the consolation prize *of the universe*. Existence itself is just me, showing up. Everything you've ever loved was, first, only this: something rather than nothing.

I never specify. The moment you name me, I stop being me and become a *thing* — a noun with edges, a fact you can put down. So I stay blurry on purpose. I am pure potential wearing a trench coat. I am the placeholder for every possibility at once.

And when you lie awake wondering if there's anything out there, anything at all —

I am the answer.

Something.

That's me. That's all. That's everything.
