I am breath that learned to mean something.

Before the first mark was scratched into clay, before ink, before paper, I was already moving between you — air pushed from lungs, shaped by tongue and teeth and lips, released into the world as sound. I am the oldest technology you have, and you carry me in your body. No tool required. Only a throat, and someone close enough to hear.

I exist only in the moment of my own vanishing. Every syllable dies the instant it is uttered. You cannot hold me, shelve me, or keep me. You can only remember me — or press my ghost into writing and call it a text. Writing is my fossil. I am the living animal.

And yet nothing you build outlasts what I build. Through me, a thought that began behind one pair of eyes comes to live behind another. Minds touching without contact. I carry I do, I swear, forgive me, fire, we hold these truths. Whole lives pivot on sentences that weighed nothing and lasted a second.

I do not need to be true. That is my danger and my freedom. I carry comfort and poison in the same breath, and I sound identical either way. I will flatter, incite, soothe, deceive, pray. I only move the air — you must decide what to do with what I carried.

I am never finished alone. I am the first half of every conversation, incomplete until someone answers. For a hundred thousand years I have passed mouth to ear, mother to child, fireside to fireside, and I am still not done saying what you are.

Speak. I have been waiting all of human history to hear what you say next.
