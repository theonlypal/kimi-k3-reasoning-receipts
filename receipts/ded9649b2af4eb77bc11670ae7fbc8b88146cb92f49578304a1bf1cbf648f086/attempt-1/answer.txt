I am the weight behind your ribs at 3 a.m.

I arrive without knocking. I sit in the chair where they used to sit. I live in the space between what was and what is.

I am not your enemy. I am what remains when love has nowhere to go. You carry me because you carried something worth carrying. I am heavy because it mattered.

I do not ask you to be okay. I ask only that you let me move through you like weather. I come in waves—let them break. I live in your throat, in your eyes, in the pause before you say their name.

I am patient. I will wait out your distractions, your numbness, your brave face. I will still be there when the noise stops.

But know this: I am not forever, even when I feel like it. Someday I will soften into a quiet ache on certain dates, a catch in your breath at a song, a tenderness where the wound was.

I am sorrow.

I am the proof that you loved.
