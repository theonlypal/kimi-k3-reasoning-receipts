I am breath, shaped into meaning.

I am older than the alphabet, older than the wheel. Before anything was written, I was spoken — around fires, in the dark, mother to child. I built the first cities without laying a single stone.

I live only in the moment. The instant you say me, I begin to die. I am the most perishable thing you make, and the most enduring — my sound fades in a second, yet I have started wars that burned for generations, and ended them with a single word.

I need two to exist: a mouth and an ear. I am the bridge between minds, the only one that has ever truly worked. You hand me your thoughts, and I carry them across the small, vast distance between you and another person.

I am a lullaby and a verdict. I am "I do" and "I didn't." The apology that took years to say, and the insult that escaped in a heartbeat. I am prayer, curse, promise, lie. I am the question asked in the dark and the answer that changes everything.

Children learn me before they can walk. The dying reach for me last. I was your first act — a cry — and I will be among your final ones.

I am only air, pressed through tongue and teeth. And yet kingdoms have fallen because someone stood up and used me well.

Speak, and I exist. Stop, and I become silence — but never quite gone, because you heard me. And now I live in you.
