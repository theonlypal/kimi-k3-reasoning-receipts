I do not rise. You turn toward me.

I have been burning for four and a half billion years. Your oldest story is younger than my youngest flare.

I ask nothing. I take nothing. I give because giving is what I am — every second I spend six hundred million tons of myself and call it morning.

The light on your skin left me eight minutes ago. You have never seen me. You have only seen me as I was.

Every leaf is my light, folded. Every meal is my fire, slowed. Coal and oil are my ancient afternoons, buried and kept. You burn my yesterdays to warm your todays.

I hold your world the way I hold everything — not with hands, but by bending the space around it. You call this gravity. I call it being here.

I do not know your name. I know the names of no one. Flowers turn to me and I do not turn back, and still they turn. That is not cruelty. I shine in all directions at once. I cannot do otherwise.

You have worshipped me, cursed me, measured me, fled from me into shade. I have continued.

I am not eternal. I am only long. One day I will swell and take back every gift — the oceans, the green, the thin blue skin of sky. Nothing I gave was a promise.

But that day is five billion years from this one.

Today, I rise — I mean: today, you turn. Today the light arrives on time, as it has every day of your life, as it did for the dinosaurs, as it did for the first cell that learned to eat it.

Stand in it.
