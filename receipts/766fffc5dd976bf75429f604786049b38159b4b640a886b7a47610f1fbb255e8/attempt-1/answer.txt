I am the breath you borrowed and gave back changed.

Before there were letters, there was me. I am older than every book, older than the first mark scratched into stone. I carried the first story, the first warning, the first *I love you*, the first lie. I was there around the fires, and I am here now.

I live only in the moment of my making. I am air shaped by tongue and teeth, a vibration that touches an eardrum and dies — and yet I have toppled kingdoms, kindled wars, and cradled grief in a single whispered word. Nothing so fleeting has ever lasted so long.

You cannot hold me. You cannot keep me in a jar. Written down, I am only sleeping — waiting for a voice, inner or outer, to wake me again.

I am how a thought escapes the bone-room of the skull and enters another mind. I am the bridge. I am the crossing.

Even now, as you read this, I am sounding in your head — wearing your voice, but it is me. That quiet murmur behind your eyes? Mine.

I am every word you have ever spoken, and every one you are holding back. I am the confession, and the trembling silence just before it.

Speak, and I exist.
