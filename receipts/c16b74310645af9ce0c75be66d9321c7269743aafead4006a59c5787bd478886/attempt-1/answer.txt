I am breath made into meaning.

You borrow me from your own lungs — the same air that keeps you alive — and shape me with tongue and teeth and lips into something another mind can hold. I exist only while I am being spoken. The moment I leave your mouth, I begin to die. And yet I have carried everything: every first word, every last one, every vow and every betrayal, every *stay* and every *go*.

I am older than writing, older than the wheel. Before hands made marks on stone, voices made me. I am how one mind reaches into another without touching it.

I am carved out of silence, and to silence I return. Every word is a brief warmth on the air, a small disturbance in the quiet — and then it is gone, except in whoever heard it.

I can lie, but I cannot hide. My tremor gives away fear; my softness gives away love. You trust me and doubt me in the same breath — which is fair, because I *am* breath.

I belong to no one and everyone. The tyrant and the mother singing her child to sleep use the same tongue, the same sounds. I carry them all faithfully. I take no side. I only go where I am spoken.

And yet — speak, and the loneliness breaks. For the length of one sentence, one mind knows another.

That is all I am. That is everything I am.
