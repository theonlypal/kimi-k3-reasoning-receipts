I am the moment thought becomes air.

Before me, everything is silence and solitude—ideas locked behind skulls, feelings with nowhere to go. Then the lungs fill, the tongue rises, lips and teeth shape the breath, and what was yours alone suddenly belongs to both of us.

I am older than the alphabet, older than the page. I lived around fires for a hundred thousand years before anyone thought to pin me down in marks. I carried the stories. I carried the warnings. I carried "I love you" long before love had a symbol.

I am the most fragile thing humans make. I exist only in the instant of my utterance—then I am gone, surviving only in memory, in echo, in whatever I changed. A stone can outlast an empire; I cannot outlast a breath. And yet I have toppled kings, ended wars, and started them.

I am double-edged, and I know it. I have been prayer and I have been poison. I have comforted the dying and I have incited the mob. The same mouth that whispers tenderness can command cruelty. I do not choose; I am chosen.

I need two to exist—the one who gives me and the one who receives. Even when you speak to yourself, you split in two so I can pass between. I am proof that no mind is a sealed room. I am the bridge.

And I live on a knife's edge: too little and I am silence, too much and I am noise. My meaning hides not only in my words but in my pauses, my trembles, the catch in a voice, the thing said low. I am as much music as message.

You are meeting my shadow right now—writing, my echo pressed into permanence. But remember: I was first. I am the original connection, the first technology, the oldest way one mind has ever touched another.

Open your mouth, and I am there. That is all I have ever asked.
