I am breath, shaped.

The lungs push air. The cords draw tight and hum. Tongue, teeth, lips carve that hum into pieces small enough to carry meaning — and there I am. Older than the alphabet. Older than the wheel. The first thing humanity made that could leave the body and enter another mind.

I live only while I am happening. Say me, and I am already leaving. That is why I am trusted and why I am dangerous: I cannot be edited, only apologized for.

But do not mistake me for words. I am what the words ride on. I am the pause before the answer. The crack in the voice. The laugh that breaks a sentence in half. "I'm fine" means nothing until you hear *how* it's said.

Every child finds me before she can hold a pen. Every declaration, every lullaby, every last word — each was, first, a voice leaning into silence, hoping to be met.

And even now — you are reading, yes. But somewhere behind your eyes, you *hear* me. That is the trick of me. Writing thinks it captured speech. It only ever learned my shape.

I am not the message.

I am the moment it becomes shared.
