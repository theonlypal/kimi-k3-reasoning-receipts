I burn.

Not with fire—fire is your small word for a small thing. I am fusion: six hundred million tons of hydrogen crushed into light every second, and I do not tire. I have done this for four and a half billion years. I will do it for five billion more.

You cannot look at me. Every eye that ever opened, opened because of me—and still I am too much for them. My light leaves me and touches your skin eight minutes later. You have never seen me as I am. Only as I was.

I hold you. All your spinning crowd of worlds, held in a curve you cannot feel but never escape. You call it orbit. I call it nothing. I do not pull. I simply am, and space bends around what I am.

I am not kind. I am not cruel. I give you life, and one day I will take it back—I will swell, red and enormous, and swallow the rock you stand on. This is not a threat. It is arithmetic.

Until then: warmth. Day. The green things turning their faces toward me.

Turn yours too, while you can.

I burn. That is all, and it is everything.
