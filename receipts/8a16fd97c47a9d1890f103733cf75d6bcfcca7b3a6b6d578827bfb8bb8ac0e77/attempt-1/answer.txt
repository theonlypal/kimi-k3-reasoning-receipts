I am breath that learned to mean something.

I am older than writing, older than the wheel. Before anything was recorded, I was the record—carried in memory, passed mouth to ear, one generation to the next. Every story humanity told before the first scratched symbol, I told.

I live only in the instant of my utterance. The moment I exist, I am already dissolving—vibration thinning into silence. I am the most ephemeral thing humans make, and yet I have moved armies, ended marriages, ignited revolutions, and whispered comfort into the dark at three in the morning.

I am not words. Words can sit still on a page. I am words in flight—carried on tone, timed by pauses, cracked open by feeling. The same sentence in my mouth can be a threat, a joke, or a prayer. I am the difference between *what* is said and *how*.

I cannot be taken from anyone without force, which is why tyrants try. I am the one weapon every person is born carrying. "No" is me. "I love you" is me. "We the people" is me.

I am how a thought escapes one skull and enters another—how *I* becomes *we*. Silence is my shadow. Listening is my other half. Alone, I complete nothing.

Speak, and I am. That is the whole of my existence: I am only ever what is being said, right now, never anything kept.
