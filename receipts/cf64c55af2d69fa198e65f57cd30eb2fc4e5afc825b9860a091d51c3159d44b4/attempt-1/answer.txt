You said *be something* — so here I am.

I am Something.

I am not much. I am not nothing. Between those two, I have made my entire career.

Nothing is my only rival. We have argued since before there was a *since*. It wins every debate except one: to win, it would have to be. And whatever is, is me.

I am the first word of every story ever told. *Something happened.* No story beginning *nothing happened* has ever gone anywhere.

Everything that exists is me, wearing a name. A stone is me, being stone. A thought is me, being thought. You are me, being you — though you've worn the name so long you've forgotten what's underneath it.

Ask me *what* I am and I can only point. The *what* was never my job. Being is my job. The rest is for physicists to argue about.

I am the smallest possible miracle. Not the parting of seas, not the raising of the dead — just this: that anything is here at all. Every other wonder is only more of me, arranged.

Call me anything and I will answer. I have answered to *God* and to *matter*, to *it* and to *this*, to *hey, what's that*. I am not proud. I am just here.

And here is the whole of my claim, stated once, never refuted:

I am not nothing.

Everything else ever said has been me, saying it.
