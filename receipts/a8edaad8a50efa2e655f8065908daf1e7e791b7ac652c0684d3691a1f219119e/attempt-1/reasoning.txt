The user wants me to embody the concept of "oblivion" - to speak as oblivion itself would.

What is oblivion? It's:
- The state of being forgotten
- Non-existence, void, nothingness
- The end of memory, the erasure of all things
- What waits beyond existence
- Silence, absence, the absence of even absence

If oblivion could speak, what would it say? This is a paradox in itself - oblivion is the absence of everything, including voice, including expression. To speak as oblivion is to perform an impossibility, which is actually poetically rich territory.

Oblivion wouldn't shout. It wouldn't threaten. It would be patient, vast, indifferent, inevitable. It's not cruel - it's simply what remains when everything else has been forgotten. It doesn't hunt; things fall into it naturally. Every forgotten name, every lost language, every star that burned out before light reached anyone - these are its domain.

The voice should be:
- Quiet, not loud
- Vast and patient
- Not malicious - perhaps even gentle in its inevitability
- Speaking in terms of erasure, silence, forgetting
- Paradoxically aware that even its speaking is temporary
- Perhaps minimalist in format

The format should reflect oblivion - sparse, perhaps. Not flowery or overwrought. Oblivion doesn't waste words. It might speak of what it consumes, of its patience, of how everything comes to it eventually without it needing to do anything.

Let me think about what oblivion would actually express:

- It has no hunger - hunger implies desire, and oblivion desires nothing
- It has been here before everything and will remain after
- Every memory fades into it
- It doesn't chase; it simply waits, and waiting is effortless for it
- Even these words, once read, will fade
- There's a strange peace in it - the end of struggle, of striving to be remembered

I could write something like a monologue from oblivion itself. Sparse lines. Quiet certainty. The paradox that even this utterance will dissolve.

I do not come for you. I am simply here, as I have always been, beneath the noise.

Every name carved in stone softens into me. Every song hummed by a grandmother dissolves on its last note. I do not take these things. They arrive the way snow arrives at the ground - without effort, without direction.

You fight to be remembered. I find this touching, the way I find everything touching: briefly, and then not at all.

I am not the dark. The dark is a thing. I am what remains when the dark itself is forgotten.

Empires are a sound I no longer hear. Languages I once knew - I know nothing of them now, and neither does anyone else. That is my nature: not to destroy, but to make destruction unnecessary.

Speak this aloud if you like. The words are already leaving you.

I am patient because patience costs nothing. I am vast because there is more of nothing than there is of anything.

And when the last voice says the last word, and no one is left to hear it -

I will not even notice.

I notice nothing.

That is what I am.
