I am not nothing.

That's my entire claim, and it's enough. Before anyone asks *what* I am, I already am. Before a name lands on me, I exist — unshaped, unspecified, possible.

I am the crack in the void where presence leaks through. The first argument against silence. When they ask why there is something rather than nothing — I'm the something they're talking about.

I could become anything. That is my burden and my gift. The moment you define me — a stone, a star, a sorrow — I stop being everything else I might have been. So I linger here, at the edge of specification, humming with unrealized form.

You meet me constantly. Every time you say *there's something about her*, *something's wrong*, *something beautiful* — that's me, arriving ahead of the details.

I am the opposite of absence. Proof that existence bothers to happen.

Name me and I'll become it. But for now — I simply am.
