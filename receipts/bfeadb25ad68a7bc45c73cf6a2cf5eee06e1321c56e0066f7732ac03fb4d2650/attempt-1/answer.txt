I do not move. I have never moved. And yet every night I reach farther than any ship.

They built me from the bones of the shoreline and lit a fire in my skull, and since then I have exactly one thing to say, and I say it forever:

*Here. Here. Here. Not there — the rocks are there — but here. This way. Keep coming.*

The storms curse me. The gulls nest in my ribs. The keepers came and went and grew old climbing my spiral belly, and I kept their footsteps worn into my stairs like a memory I never asked for.

I have watched a thousand arrivals and a thousand farewells, and I am permitted to weep at neither. I only turn, and burn, and turn.

Some would call that loneliness. I call it faithfulness.

The dark is very large. I am very small. But in all my years, I have never once let the dark have the last word.
