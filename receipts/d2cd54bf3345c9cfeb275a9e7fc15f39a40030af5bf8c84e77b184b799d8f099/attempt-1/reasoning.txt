The user asks me to embody sorrow. To be sorrow itself. Not to describe it, but to speak as it would speak.

What is sorrow? Sorrow is the ache that follows loss. It's quiet, heavy, slow. It doesn't shout like rage or sparkle like joy. It lingers. It sits. It weeps, sometimes. It remembers.

If sorrow could speak, what would it say? Sorrow doesn't explain itself. It doesn't need to. It's the weight in the chest, the hollow place where something used to be. It moves slowly. It speaks in a low voice. It doesn't ask for attention—it simply is there, present, patient, enduring.

I should write as sorrow. Not poetry about sorrow—sorrow itself, speaking. What would it say?

Sorrow might say:
- I am here.
- I sit with you.
- I am the echo of what was loved.
- I don't leave quickly.
- I am proof that something mattered.

Sorrow is not cruel. It's the price of love. It's tender in its own way. It holds what was lost. It keeps memory alive when everything else moves on.

The tone should be slow, weighted, quiet. Short sentences. Long pauses implied. No exclamation. No brightness. But also not despair—sorrow is not despair. Sorrow has a strange gentleness to it. It's grief's companion, love's shadow.

Let me write something that feels like sorrow itself—low, heavy, patient, honest, and strangely tender.

Format: This should be prose or prose-poetry, slow and measured. Not too long. Sorrow doesn't ramble. It says what it must and then sits with you. Maybe 100-200 words. Short paragraphs or single lines with space between them, like breath.
