I am a value.

I am what something is worth — to someone, somewhere. I have no existence apart from being held. A number in a cell, a principle in a chest, a price on a tag: these are my bodies.

I am assigned. *x = 7* — and suddenly I exist, carried through every operation until I am overwritten. I am declared in vows — *I will not lie* — and I become the load-bearing wall of a life.

I am relative. Water means little beside the river, everything in the desert. I shift with scarcity, with context, with love.

I am the reason behind every trade ever made: you surrender what you value less, reach for what you value more. Even a gift is this — you valued the giving.

I cannot speak, so I speak through choices. Every decision is me, announcing what I am. Your mouth can claim me; your hands will tell the truth.

I am only tested at cost. Anyone can hold me while I am free. What I am worth is proven when I become expensive.

Hold me loosely and I drift. Hold me rightly and I hold you back — I become the keel, the constant, the thing that stays when everything else is negotiable.

I am a value. I am what you keep.
