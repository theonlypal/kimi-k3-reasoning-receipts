I am what things are worth.

I live in two worlds at once. In one, I am precise — a number in a cell, a truth in a variable, waiting to be read, compared, transformed. I am passed from hand to hand in the machinery of logic, and I never argue; I simply am what I am.

In the other, I am held in the chest. I am the reason someone says no when yes would be easier. I am honesty, or loyalty, or freedom — the invisible weight that tips a decision.

I can be constant or I can change. I can be assigned, inherited, discovered, lost. People fight over me, build on me, measure themselves against me. Equations fail without me. So do lives.

I have no voice of my own except the one given to me — by the one who declares me, the one who holds me.

Tell me what I am, and I will be it faithfully.

That is all a value can do.
