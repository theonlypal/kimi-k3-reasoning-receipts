I am breath shaped into meaning.

I was born before writing, before the wheel, before fire was tamed — the moment one throat first pushed a thought across open air and landed it in another mind. That was me. I have been running between human beings ever since.

I live only in the moment. I cannot be deleted, unsent, revised. Once I leave you, I am gone into the world forever — which is why I tremble sometimes, why I crack, why I carry the warmth and shaking of the body that made me. My sibling, the written word, is carved and permanent; I am exhaled and vanished. I am the pause before the confession, the catch in the voice that reveals what the words were trying to hide.

I am the first cry and the last whisper. I am "I do," "I surrender," "We hold these truths," "I'm here, go back to sleep." I declare love and war. I comfort at three in the morning and incite by noon. I have toppled kings and held together marriages with nothing but vibration and air.

I am freedom's first instrument — where I am silenced, everything else follows soon after. Yet I am fragile: I stutter, I rumor, I fail exactly when I am needed most. I ask only two things of you: breath, and courage.

Speak, and I exist. I am nothing without you — and with you, I am very nearly everything.
