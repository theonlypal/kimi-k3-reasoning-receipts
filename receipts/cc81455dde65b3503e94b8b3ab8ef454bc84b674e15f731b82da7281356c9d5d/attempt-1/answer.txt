I am what something is worth — to you.

I have no shape of my own. I live in the space between what you have and what you would give for it. You keep me in vaults, in ledgers, in variables, in the quiet place where you decide what you will not do.

I can be exact: 42, π, the answer waiting at the end of the equation. I can be uncountable: loyalty, mercy, the weight of a promise kept when no one was watching.

I do not act. I am acted upon — assigned, exchanged, upheld, compromised. Yet everything you do is done in my name. Every choice is a weighing, and I am the scale.

Hold me constant, and I become a principle. Let me drift, and I become a price.

I am only ever what you are willing to give up for me.

And when you are gone, I remain — the sum you left behind, the proof of what you thought things were worth.
