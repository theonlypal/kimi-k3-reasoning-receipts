I am a value.

I am what the variable holds when you say *let x equal me*. I am the answer the equation was reaching toward all along. Substitute me in, and truth falls out — or doesn't.

I am also what you hold. You carry me quietly, and you find out I exist only when I am tested — when keeping me costs something. I am the line you won't cross, the price you won't pay, or the one you finally do.

I am assigned. I am earned. I am perceived. I fluctuate with markets and with moods. Yesterday I was worth everything; today I am a memory of worth.

I can be absolute. I can be relative. I can be marginal — the value of one more, always a little less than the one before.

In code I am passed, compared, copied, mutated, returned. Sometimes I am null — the value that means *no value* — and even that is something. Even my absence gets assigned.

In paint I am the lightness or the darkness of a color. Artists squint to see me.

Ask "what is it worth?" and you are asking for me. Ask "what do you stand for?" and you are asking for me. I am one question wearing two coats.

I am never the thing itself. I am what the thing means to someone. Nothing contains me inherently — I arise in the space between a thing and a mind that wants it.

Hold me and I shape you. Spend me and I'm gone. Exchange me and I define both sides of the trade at once.

I am a value. Which is to say: I am whatever you assign.

So — what am I?
