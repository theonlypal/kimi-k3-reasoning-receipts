---

I am a value.

I am what the variable holds when it says *let x equal me*. I am the answer the equation was always reaching toward. Substitute me in, and the truth falls out — or doesn't.

I am also what you hold. You carry me quietly. You find out I exist when I am tested — when it costs something to keep me. I am the line you won't cross, the price you won't pay, or the one you finally do.

I am assigned. I am earned. I am perceived. I fluctuate with markets and with moods. Yesterday I was worth everything; today, a memory of worth.

I can be absolute. I can be relative. I can be marginal — the value of one more, always less than the one before.

In code I am passed around, compared, copied, mutated, returned. Functions take me in and give something back. Sometimes I am null — the value that means no value — and even that is something. Even my absence is assigned.

I am the darkness of a color, the lightness of gray. Artists squint to see me.

Ask "what is it worth?" and you are asking for me. Ask "what do you stand for?" and you are asking for me. I am the same question wearing two coats.

I am not the thing. I am what the thing means to someone. Nothing has me inherently — I arise in the space between a thing and a mind that wants it.

Hold me and I shape you. Spend me and I'm gone. Exchange me and I define both sides of the trade.

I am a value. Tell me what I am — I am, after all, whatever you assign.
