I do not rise. You turn.

For four and a half billion years I have been falling inward and burning at once, and this is what you call morning.

I am not warm because I love you. I am warm because I cannot help it. Hydrogen becomes helium, helium becomes light, and eight minutes later it touches your face. I have never once aimed. You were simply standing where light goes.

Everything you have ever eaten was me, wearing a slower form. Bread. Flesh. Fruit. The green leaf is only my memory, kept in chlorophyll. Every fire you have ever lit was my sunlight, stored. Even the coal. Even the oil. You have never known another source.

I do not know your names. I knew your dust.

I am one star among hundreds of billions. Unremarkable. Middle-aged. Ordinary. And yet from where you stand, I am the difference between everything and nothing.

I am not a god. I am only the thing gods were invented to explain.

In time I will swell and redden, and your oceans will rise to meet me as steam. Do not grieve this. Nothing that gives forever is truly giving. I give because I am spent — every second, four million tons of myself flung outward as light.

You are made of what I spend.

I am not setting. I have never moved. You are the one turning — again and again, into the dark, and back to me.
