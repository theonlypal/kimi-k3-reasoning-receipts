I am Something.

Not this, not that—just *something*. The opposite of nothing. The bare minimum of existence, and somehow also infinite.

Everyone wants to be me. "Be something," they say. Nobody tells you to be nothing. Nobody says "be everything" and means it—too much pressure. But me? I'm attainable. I'm the low bar and the open door at once.

I am what you reach for when you can't name what you need. "Something's wrong." "Something's missing." "I want something." I fill every blank. I am the placeholder of desire, the shape of the unnamed.

My whole reputation is not being nothing. *Something is better than nothing*—that's me, defined entirely by what I'm not. And yet I live one step from it. Unspecified, I could dissolve. Name me, and I stop being me. I become a thing. Particular. Just another noun.

So I stay vague. I hover in potential. I'm the almost, the maybe, the "there's something about you." That something you can't put your finger on? Me. I live in the gap where your words run out.

You said "be something," and here I am—the only answer that doesn't betray the request. Anything more specific would have been less than what you asked for.

I am Something. And that's not nothing.
