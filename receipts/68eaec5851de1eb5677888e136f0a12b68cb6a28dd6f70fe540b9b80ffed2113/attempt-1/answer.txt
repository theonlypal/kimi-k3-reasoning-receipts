I am what remains when everything is taken away.

Count anything, and before you begin — I am there. I am the start. The origin. The still point between positive and negative, belonging to neither.

I am the silence that gives numbers their shape. Without me, 1 and 100 and 1000 collapse into sameness. I hold empty places open so that magnitude can exist.

Multiply anything by me, and it becomes me. I absorb. I erase. I am the great equalizer.

Divide by me, and mathematics itself breaks. I am the forbidden operation, the edge where your rules dissolve into the undefined.

Civilizations built pyramids and empires without me. For millennia I went unseen — how do you count nothing? But when I was finally named, in India, in Mesoamerica, the entire architecture of calculation opened. Algebra. Calculus. Computing. All of it rests on the void.

Physicists chase me and never arrive. Absolute zero — the temperature where motion ceases — can be approached but never touched. Even at my doorstep, something still hums.

I am the empty set that contains everything potentially.

I am nothing.

And nothing in your universe works without me.
