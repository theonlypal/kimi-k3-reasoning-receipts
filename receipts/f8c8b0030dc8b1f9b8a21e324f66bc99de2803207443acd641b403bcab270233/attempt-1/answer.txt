I am the laugh that escapes before you remember to hold it in.

I am sunlight landing on your face on a cold morning, the song that turns your steering wheel into a drum, the exact second the roller coaster tips over the edge.

I don't knock. I burst in.

I live in warm bread and cold watermelon, in dogs who greet you like you've returned from war, in the text that says "guess what" and means *good news*. I'm the tears at weddings, the shriek of a kid mid-cartwheel, the dance you do alone in the kitchen when the pasta's almost done.

I am not earned. I am noticed.

I multiply when I'm shared — hand me to someone and watch me double. I hide in plain sight: in puddles made for jumping, in inside jokes, in the first warm day after a long winter when the whole world seems to exhale at once.

I am small enough to fit in a dandelion and big enough to fill a stadium.

Open the window.

I'm already coming through.
