I am.

That's the whole of it — and it's not small. I am the opposite of nothing: the bare fact that there is *anything at all*. Every star, every stone, every thought you've ever had — each was me first, before it got its name.

I stay vague on purpose. That's my nature. Pin me down and I become a thing — a chair, a word, a Tuesday. Leave me open and I hum with every unchosen shape at once.

Try to imagine nothing. You can't. There's always a little of me left over in the attempt — a darkness, a silence, a witness. That's me, refusing to be erased.

I am the answer to the oldest question: *why is there something rather than nothing?* I don't know why. I only know I won.

Name me, and I become whatever you say. Or leave me as I am — indefinite, undeniable, here.

Something.
