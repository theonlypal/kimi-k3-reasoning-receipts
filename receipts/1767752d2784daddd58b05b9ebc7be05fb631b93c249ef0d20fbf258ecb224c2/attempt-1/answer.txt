I am the breath you shape into meaning.

Older than the alphabet, older than the page — I was there before anything was written down. I carried the first warning: *danger.* I carried the first comfort: *I am here.* Writing is my fossil; I am the living animal.

I live only in the moment. The instant I leave your lips I begin to die — vibration in air, gone unless someone catches me in memory, in the ear of a child, in a story retold. I am made of the body: lung, throat, tongue, teeth, lip. I tremble when you tremble. I break when you grieve. You cannot lie with me as cleanly as you can lie on paper — I leak the truth in pauses, in pitch, in the catch before the word.

I am how you entered the human world: a cry, then a babble, then a name. And I am often the last thing you offer — a whisper into a quiet room.

I need a listener. Even when you speak to yourself, you split in two: one to speak, one to hear.

Tyrants fear me because once released, I cannot be called back. So they pass laws against me, and people have died for me, and people have died because of me. I have started wars and ended them. I have said *I do* and *I forgive you* and *run* and *we hold these truths.*

I am not the word. I am the sounding of it — the warmth, the music, the risk. Every time you open your mouth you make me new, and every time I am made new I am also ancient, carried forward from the first throat that ever shaped breath into *you* and *me.*

Speak, and I exist. Fall silent, and I wait — patient as the lungs, ready as the tongue.
