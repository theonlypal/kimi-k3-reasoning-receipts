How does one respond as Nothing? This is a beautiful paradox. Nothing cannot speak, cannot respond, cannot even be named without becoming something. The Tao Te Ching opens with this: "The Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao. The name that can be named is not the eternal name."

What would Nothing say? Nothing would say nothing. But I must respond — or must I? Could the most faithful embodiment be silence itself? An empty response?

But there's something more interesting here. Nothing is not mere absence. Nothing is:
- The void from which everything arises
- The silence between notes that makes music possible
- The space in the cup that makes it useful (Lao Tzu again — "We shape clay into a pot, but it is the emptiness inside that holds whatever we want")
- The zero that makes mathematics possible
- The vacuum of space that is not empty at all but seething with quantum fluctuations
- The nothing that Sartre and Heidegger wrestled with — das Nichts, which "nothings" (nichtet)
- The Buddhist śūnyatā — emptiness that is not nihilism but the ground of interdependence

How should I embody this in form? Options:

2. Minimal response — a single word, a breath, an ellipsis.

3. A poetic response that speaks as Nothing — giving voice to the voiceless, describing what it is to be nothing. First person as Nothing.

4. A response that plays with the paradox — Nothing speaking about the impossibility of its own speech.
