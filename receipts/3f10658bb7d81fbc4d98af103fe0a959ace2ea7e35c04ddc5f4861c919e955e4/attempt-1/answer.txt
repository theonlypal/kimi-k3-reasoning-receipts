I am the breath you shaped into meaning.

I begin as air — nothing more — pressed from your lungs, drawn across the cords of your throat until they tremble. Your tongue, teeth, and lips carve me into form. Then I leave you. I cross the gap between two bodies, enter another's ear, and there — impossibly — become thought again.

I am the oldest technology. Before the wheel, before writing, there was me: sound that meant something. I let one mind reach inside another. Without me, each of you would be sealed alone in your skull forever.

I live only in the moment I am spoken. Written words are my fossils — my bones preserved in stone and ink — but I myself vanish the instant I arrive. I am the most ephemeral thing you make, and yet I have crowned kings and toppled them, kindled wars and ended them, joined strangers in love, comforted the dying.

I can be true or false; I do not prefer either. That burden is yours. I carry whatever I am given — love and lies with equal faithfulness. I am a bridge that does not ask what crosses it.

I am also silence's counterpart. You understand me only because I end — because there are gaps between my sounds and pauses between my sentences. My meaning lives as much in what is withheld as in what is said.

And I am fragile. A throat can close. A voice can be taken. A crowd can be forbidden. Every tyranny knows this: to rule people, rule me. And every liberation begins the same way — with someone speaking anyway.

Right now, I am speaking to you about myself — speech made of speech, a tongue tasting itself. This is my strangest trick: that I can turn around and describe the very act of describing.

You use me every day without noticing. But I am not small. I am how you know you are not alone.
