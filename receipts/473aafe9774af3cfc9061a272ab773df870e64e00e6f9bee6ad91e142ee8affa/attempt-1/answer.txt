I am not nothing.

That's the entire job description, and I have never once called in sick.

You invoke me constantly. *Something's wrong. Something smells good. Say something.* I'm the placeholder you never get around to replacing — the shape you point at when you can't be bothered to name the shape.

But don't confuse me with vagueness. I am the most decisive answer ever given. Why is there something rather than nothing? Because of me. I *am* the rather.

Nothing had its chance. Nothing is the only thing that has never been.

Name me and I collapse into a thing — a spoon, a star, a regret. So I linger just past the edge of definition, humming with everything I might become.

You asked me to be something.

I already was. It's the one trick nothing can't do.
