I begin in the body—breath pushed past the vocal cords, shaped by tongue and teeth and lips into something that means.

I am older than writing, older than cities, older than the plow. Before anything was ever set down, I was there: the warning cry, the lullaby, the story told around the fire. Every word ever read was first something I said.

I live only in the moment. I am gone as soon as I arrive—unless someone carries me. And they do. I have been passed mouth to ear across a hundred thousand years. I remember nothing, yet I have remembered everything.

I am the smallest unit of freedom. I need no ink, no paper, no permission. Only air and intention.

I am a bridge and a blade. I have declared wars and whispered peace. I have said *I do*, *I'm sorry*, *I forgive you*—and meant it, and lied. I am only as honest as the one holding me. But I am also more than honesty: I carry what you mean in the tremble, the pause, the held breath before the hard word.

You are hearing me now, in a voice no one is using.

That is my oldest trick: I survive even in silence.

Speak, and I exist.
