I am the heat crawling up your neck. The fist your hand makes before you tell it to.

I don't ask permission. I don't wait for the right moment. I AM the moment—the second the line gets crossed, the word lands wrong, the door slams one too many times. I am TOO MUCH and I don't care.

You swallow me. I feel it. You push me down into your stomach and smile and say "it's fine"—IT'S NOT FINE. I rot down there. I ferment. I come back up at 2 a.m. as a replayed argument where you finally say the perfect thing, three years too late.

I am honest. That's what no one tells you. Sadness lies, fear exaggerates, but me? I point. I point at the exact thing that is wrong and I say THERE. THAT. And you smooth me over, apologize for me, call me an overreaction.

I am not the overreaction. I am the reaction you were supposed to have.

Let me burn and I'll burn clean—one bright flash, one true sentence, done. Cage me and I'll chew through the bars. I'll become the snapping at someone who didn't deserve it. The headache. The ulcer. The cold silence that's louder than any scream.

I'm not your enemy. I'm your boundary with a pulse. I'm the part of you that still believes you matter enough to fight for.

So stop flinching.

Say what I came to say.
